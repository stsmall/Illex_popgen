# G3 referee report: "Cryptic genomic variation in a panmictic squid: an ancient inversion supergene and genome-wide signatures of positive selection in *Illex illecebrosus*"

Reviewed: `main.tex` and `supplement.tex` (source as of commit `449f46a`, "Drop ABC-SMC"), the input tables
(`software_table.tex`, `inversion_gene_table.tex`, `pi_blocks_table.tex`, `sampling_table.tex`), main-text Figs 1 to 6
and Supplementary Figs S14, S25 and S26. Locations are given as `file:line` in the LaTeX source (paragraphs are long
single lines, so a short quotation is added where it helps). "S" before a number means the supplement.

**Recommendation: major revision.**

---

## Summary of the claims

The authors re-map 633 published whole genomes of *I. illecebrosus* (Baker et al. 2025) to a new conspecific
chromosome-level assembly and analyse 619 individuals. They report (1) range-wide panmixia with no geographic
outlier SNPs, (2) a ~19 Mb inversion on chromosome 2 (karyotypes 223/299/97, derived 2a at 0.602), polarised as 2a
derived from phased haplotype assemblies of the reference individual and two outgroups, (3) that the inversion is
non-neutral, with 2a less diverse than its frequency predicts, ABC-RF favouring balancing selection (P = 0.83) over
neutral drift and directional selection, and NPE dating the recombination barrier to ~390 ka with a soft start
(p0 ~ 0.05) and an equilibrium p* ~ 0.60 held for ~310 ky, (4) a 12-fold expansion to Ne ~ 6.8 million with a
Kingman rather than multiple-merger genealogy, (5) 627 Tier-1 sweep calls and 24 cross-method concordant regions
enriched for neural development and photoreception, and (6) achiasmate female meiosis inferred from Z versus
autosome LD in males.

## Overall assessment

This is a large and useful dataset, and several parts are done with real care: the polarisation from the phased
haplotypes of a heterozygous reference individual plus two outgroups, the depth-equalised karyotype check, the
permutation null for per-SNP FST, the honest description of diploS/HIC miscalibration, the masking and depth
classification of the 22 anomalous-diversity blocks, and the calibration (SBC/TARP) of the NPE. The removal of ABC-SMC
has simplified the inversion section.

The main problems are these. (i) The headline inversion conclusion (balancing selection, neutral drift excluded) is
carried almost entirely by the prior probability that each history ends at the present frequency, not by the genomic
statistics, and balancing selection and a stalled sweep are close to unidentifiable at this Ne. (ii) The posterior
predictive checks and the model choice use 40-kb simulations whose noise is much larger than that of the 19-Mb
observed statistics, so the checks have little power. (iii) Genotype quality is described inconsistently and could
differ between karyotype groups. (iv) The demography, recombination rate and control regions used for the inversion
differ from those used elsewhere in the paper. (v) Many numbers were not updated when karyotypes were corrected
(abstract 0.618, FST 0.37, 2.2-fold, n = 621, 19 exonic differences in nine genes, and others). (vi) Several conclusions
on the supergene, local adaptation, sweeps and achiasmy are stated more strongly than the evidence allows. The
authors' own orange `\comment{[overclaim ...]}` notes are still in the compiled text (main.tex:49, 55, 75, 178, 224,
378, 489), and most of them agree with points below.

## Major comments

### M1. Model choice for the inversion rests on prior volume, not on the data

- **supplement.tex:111, main.tex:138-144, abstract main.tex:49.** The forest alone gives D2 0.31, B 0.29, N 0.24, D1
  0.17 at the observed statistics. That is, the 39 statistics slightly favour a *stalled sweep* and do not reject
  neutral drift. The reported P(B) = 0.83 comes from multiplying by the probability that a prior draw passes the
  present-frequency filter (0.0064 for B, 0.0005-0.0006 for the others). I checked:
  0.29 × 0.0064 / (0.29 × 0.0064 + 0.31 × 0.00055 + 0.24 × 0.00055 + 0.17 × 0.00055) ≈ 0.82. The result is therefore
  a ratio of prior volumes. It depends directly on arbitrary prior choices: p* uniform on 0.3-0.95 (a narrower
  range around 0.6 raises the B acceptance rate and a wider range lowers it), t_stop = f' t_sel with f' uniform
  (which puts little mass on stopping exactly when p ≈ 0.6), the upper bound p0 ≤ 0.10 ("the largest founding
  frequency we consider biologically reasonable", supplement.tex:105), and the t_inv range. The main text half
  admits this at main.tex:140-144 ("Most of this support comes from how readily each history reaches the present
  frequency"). The abstract, Discussion (main.tex:356, 360) and title do not.
  **Requests.** (a) Report the forest-only probabilities in the main text next to the 0.83. (b) Give a
  prior-sensitivity analysis: p* on 0.05-0.95, D2 parameterised by its stop *frequency* (uniform) rather than its stop
  time, p0 up to 0.3-0.5, and a wider t_inv range. Show how P(B) changes. (c) The cross-validation in Fig S26
  assesses the forest only. Cross-validate the full procedure that produces 0.83 (forest × acceptance rate) so that
  readers can see how often it picks B when D2 or N is true.
- **Identifiability of B against D2.** With Ne between 5 × 10^5 and 9 × 10^6 (supplement.tex:102), drift after a
  sweep stops moves the frequency very little over 3 × 10^5 generations (time scale ~2Ne). So a sweep that stopped at
  ~0.6 about 310 ka gives almost the same trajectory, and the same present-day statistics, as an overdominant
  equilibrium at p* = 0.6. Fig S26 agrees: D2 is recovered in 0.14 of its mass and B in 0.36, with B confused with N
  at 0.33. **Request.** State plainly that the data cannot separate "held by balancing selection" from "rose and then
  stopped". Rewrite the title ("supergene"), abstract ("held by balancing selection at an intermediate frequency",
  "favours balancing selection over ... directional selection"), main.tex:188-189 and the Discussion heading at
  main.tex:355 to match. The genotype counts fit Hardy-Weinberg closely (expected 224/297/98 at p = 0.602 against
  223/299/97 observed). This is worth reporting: it gives no sign of strong viability overdominance at the sampled
  life stage, although it cannot exclude the weak s the posterior allows.
- **"The inversion is non-neutral" (main.tex:107-117).** The 2.1-fold diversity deficit of 2a is presented as the
  "primary evidence" of non-neutrality. A derived arrangement that drifted up from a low founding frequency also shows
  this deficit, and the forest gives N 0.24 on these statistics. What argues against N is the low probability of
  drifting from p0 ≤ 0.1 to 0.6 in the allowed time at this Ne, and that depends on the p0 and t_inv priors and on
  there being no population structure (the Limitations, main.tex:406, accept an allopatric origin is possible).
  **Request.** Retitle the paragraph and say exactly what rejects N. Show the neutral probability of reaching 0.60 as
  a function of p0 and t_inv, so readers can judge how robust the rejection is.

### M2. The soft-start model and the founding frequency

- **Fig S14 (top), main.tex:146-148, 362-374, Table 2 (main.tex:558).** The marginal posterior of log10 p0 piles up
  against the prior upper bound (0.10). The reported "founding frequency of about 0.05 (0.004-0.10)" is therefore
  partly set by the prior, as the authors' own comment at main.tex:378 notes. **Request.** Extend the p0 prior, or
  report p0 as a lower bound and stop quoting a point value in the abstract and Discussion.
- **What the soft start represents (supplement.tex:105).** A soft start places 2a at p0 ≥ 10^-3 (≥ ~1,000 copies at
  N ≈ 5 × 10^5) at t_inv, so the 2a founding lineages are an instantaneous random sample of the ancestral population.
  A structural inversion arises once, on one haplotype. Shared ancestral variation in 2a then requires gene flux
  during an early phase, recurrent formation, or secondary contact. The single-copy alternative is not a fair
  comparison because selection starts at t_inv (no neutral establishment phase) and the path is weighted by its
  establishment probability, so the "3% single-copy" share (main.tex:144, 363-364) also reflects how the model is
  built rather than the data. **Requests.** (a) Add a single-origin model with an initial neutral phase plus gene
  flux, or explain why it is equivalent. (b) Interpret t_inv as the time 2a was already at p0, not as the age of the
  inversion mutation, and say how much older the mutation could be. (c) Test multiple origins directly. If 2a was
  founded more than once, 2a haplotypes near the breakpoints should fall into deeply divergent groups. A haplotype or
  distance tree of 2a/2a individuals in the first and last megabase would answer this. "A region whose breakpoints
  are reused may also produce the same arrangement more than once" (main.tex:367-369) needs either such evidence or
  a citation for recurrent inversions with identical breakpoints.
- **Allele sharing with outgroups (main.tex:370, supplement.tex:89).** Which arrangement carries the *I. coindetii*
  allele at diagnostic sites mostly reflects how fast each arrangement sorted ancestral variation, which depends on
  its past size. It is not independent evidence about the order of the rearrangement. The 26% of sites at which 2a
  matches *I. coindetii* is a quantity the fitted model can predict. **Request.** Present the allele-sharing
  results as consistent with the fitted history (or add them as a posterior predictive check) rather than as support
  for polarity. The structural polarisation from the haplotype assemblies and *S. oualaniensis* is convincing on its
  own.

### M3. Posterior predictive checks and model choice are much noisier than the data, and the simulator is not validated

- **supplement.tex:99, 113-114, 120, main.tex:159-166.** Every simulation covers 40 kb of inversion. The observed
  statistics are means over 18.96 Mb. The authors' length test shows that the spread across draws at full length is
  0.3-1.0 of the 40-kb spread for the fitted statistics and 0.12 for the edge-to-centre ratio. A PPC with
  replicate variance up to 8 times too large will pass almost any model. The joint p = 0.51, the "no statistic below
  0.05" and the withheld edge-to-centre ratio at p = 0.99 therefore say little. The same extra noise probably explains
  the low cross-validated accuracy (0.51) of the model choice. **Requests.** (a) Repeat the PPC with full-length
  simulations (or simulate as many 40-kb blocks as the data contain and average them). (b) If possible, rebuild the
  ABC-RF reference table from longer simulations so that the statistics can discriminate. (c) As in the previous
  round of review, show that msinv reproduces a forward simulation (for example SLiM with an inversion and gene
  conversion) for the statistics used, at the fitted parameters. The headline ages depend on it, and no such check is
  in the paper.
- **Gene flux, conflicting statements.** main.tex:164-165 (and supplement.tex:120) give the shared low-frequency
  cell as observed 0.064, predicted 0.031 with gene flux (p = 0.07) and 0.035 without (p = 0.29), so the model
  *without* gene flux fits this cell better. main.tex:187-188 says "the fitted model reproduces the variants shared by
  the arrangements better with gene flux than without it". **Request.** Reconcile the two statements. Also note that
  both settings predict about half the observed value, and that the gene-flux marginal (Fig S14) has a long tail down
  to the prior lower bound, so gene flux is not clearly separated from zero.
- **Correlations.** The text (main.tex:154) gives t_sel and t_inv ρ = 0.56 but leaves out the t_sel and gene-flux
  correlation (0.42, bold in Fig S14). Please add it or explain why it is omitted.

### M4. Genotype quality, depth effects and possible technical differences between karyotype groups

- **Contradictory description of heterozygote calling.** The error model used in the inversion simulations has a
  heterozygote called homozygous with probability h = 0.64-0.69 and call rates 0.62-0.67 (supplement.tex:99). The
  Fig 6 caption (main.tex:521) explains negative FST by an *excess* of called heterozygotes (Ho/He = 1.13). Both cannot
  describe the same callset without explanation. Autosomal PC1 tracks read count at ρ = -0.99 (Fig S1), and 12 of
  the 18 karyotype disagreements are low-depth individuals (supplement.tex:82). Genotype calls clearly depend on
  depth. **Request.** Define h and Ho/He precisely, give both from the same filtered callset, and show how
  heterozygosity varies with depth.
- **Karyotype calls.** Three of the 18 reassigned individuals moved from full-depth 2a/2a to equal-depth 2+/2+, which
  is a jump between opposite homozygotes (supplement.tex:82). **Request.** Genotype the karyotypes directly from
  genotype likelihoods at the 5,432 near-fixed sites (or the 15,093 sites with ΔAF ≥ 0.7), report the posterior
  karyotype probability per individual, and say what happened to these three.
- **Asymmetry between karyotype groups in collinear sequence.** In the chr2 collinear flanks, 2+/2+ carries more
  private rare variants than 2a/2a (0.352 against 0.272 of variable sites), "which a single panmictic population does
  not produce" (supplement.tex:102). Outside the inversion the two groups should be exchangeable. An unexplained
  difference there points to a technical difference between the groups (depth, batch, sampling event or
  contamination), and the same bias would affect the within-inversion SFS, π_I/π_S and the joint-spectrum cells that
  sit at the edge of the PPC. **Requests.** Show the depth, call rate and sampling event distributions by karyotype
  group. Recompute the inversion statistics with groups matched on depth. Report π_I/π_S from the per-karyotype ANGSD
  spectra (genotype likelihoods) as well as from called genotypes. At present the 0.719 headline comes from called
  genotypes (supplement.tex:108), whereas the paper's own rule is to use genotype likelihoods for diversity
  (main.tex:435-436).

### M5. The demography, recombination rate, mutation rate and control regions used for the inversion are inconsistent with the rest of the paper

- **Three demographies.** Genome-wide moments: 547,928 → 6,808,096 over 769,519 generations (main.tex:226).
  Chromosome-2 baseline: 507,288 → 9,112,696 over 382,957 generations (main.tex:448, supplement.tex:102).
  ReLERNN was trained under Stairway Plot 2 (supplement.tex:126), whose recent Ne sits at the θ ceiling
  (main.tex:228-229). The barrier age (~390 ka) almost coincides with the onset of growth in the chr2 baseline
  (~383 ka). **Request.** Show that t_inv and t_sel are not driven by the baseline. Refit the NPE under the genome-wide
  moments demography and report the change.
- **Recombination differs by up to 18-fold.** ReLERNN gives ~0.24 cM/Mb (2.4 × 10^-9), the chr2 baseline fits
  r = 5.5 × 10^-10, GONE2 assumes 1 cM/Mb (main.tex:234), and the LD-decay fit in males gives ρ_A = 0.026 per base
  (supplement.tex:165). That ρ_A implies r ≈ 1 × 10^-9 at Ne ≈ 6.8 × 10^6, and the ReLERNN rate at the Stairway Ne
  implies a ρ 3 to 5 times higher. These rates feed the LD statistics of the inversion model, the HMM dwell lengths
  of the sweep decoder and the GONE2 Ne. **Request.** Reconcile them, or state which is used for what and why. Rerun
  GONE2 at the estimated rate (GONE2 Ne scales with the assumed rate, so 4.8-5.0 million at 1 cM/Mb is not comparable
  to the other estimates).
- **Mutation rate.** μ = 3 × 10^-9 (main.tex:170, Table 3) is given no source. Every absolute age depends on it.
  Please cite it and show the ages for a plausible range.
- **Control regions on chromosome 2.** The baseline avoids "a stretch of doubled diversity at chr2:24.1-35.9 Mb"
  (supplement.tex:102). The collinear control used for the fixed differences (main.tex:208), the zero-fold test
  (supplement.tex:94), the *I. argentinus* reference-bias calibration (supplement.tex:89) and the dashed d_xy in Fig 3c
  is chr2:10-30 Mb, which overlaps that stretch. In addition, a 12-Mb region at twice the chromosome median meets the
  definition of an anomalous-diversity block (supplement.tex:203), yet chr2 has no block in Table S9. **Request.** Use
  one justified control throughout, and explain why chr2:24.1-35.9 Mb is not among the blocks (or add it).
- The chr2 flank diversity is given as π = 0.0061 (main.tex:135) and 0.0062 (supplement.tex:102). Why chr2 collinear
  sequence is a third less diverse than the autosomes deserves a sentence.

### M7. Statements about the supergene, local adaptation and selection on the arrangement go beyond the evidence

- **"Supergene" and "ancient" (title, main.tex:49, 172-204, 360).** Thousands of near-fixed differences across the
  inversion are expected for any old inversion with suppressed recombination, whether neutral or selected. Absence of
  GO enrichment among 29 GO-annotated genes is absence of evidence. Neither shows that selection acts "on the
  arrangement as a whole rather than on a single locus" (main.tex:49, 204, 360). No phenotype is known, and
  "supergene" normally implies linked loci affecting a phenotype. At ~390 ka, and younger than the split from
  *I. argentinus*, "ancient" needs a reference point. **Request.** Use "long-lived inversion polymorphism" or similar,
  and present selection on the arrangement as a hypothesis.
- **"Not locally adaptive" (main.tex:75, 176-178, Fig 1 caption main.tex:489).** Only latitude, division and capture
  depth were tested. The depth slope is p = 0.09 and other environmental axes (temperature, season, cohort) are not
  examined. The per-SNP null uses 20 permutations, which limits the resolution of the p-values, and there is no power
  analysis. **Request.** Say "no evidence of geographic differentiation", add a power simulation (what level of
  divergent selection or local FST could have been detected), increase the permutations, and finish the incomplete
  sentence at main.tex:176-178.
- **"Unambiguous signature of expansion" (main.tex:224).** Tajima's D alone cannot separate growth from linked
  selection or sweepstakes reproduction, as the Discussion (main.tex:394) itself says. Reword.

### M8. Sweep scan: the Tier-1 calls are uncalibrated, and the enrichment analyses lack proper nulls

- **Tier-1 rests on the raw classifier.** The Discussion says "We therefore did not interpret the raw classifier
  output directly" (main.tex:387), yet Tier-1 is an HMM over the prior-corrected class posteriors of that same
  classifier (main.tex:285-293), which calls 91% of the genome linked-selected. The supplement says Tier-1 "calls
  are stratified outliers by construction and have no comparable [FDR] estimate" (supplement.tex:192-193). The
  abstract still reports "627 candidate sweep calls". **Request.** Present Tier-1 as an exploratory ranking with no
  error rate, or give it one, for example with the same circular-shift approach applied to the HMM centres.
- **GO enrichment for window-based calls.** Genes overlapping 100-kb windows are biased toward long genes, and
  neural-development genes are long in most metazoans. Fisher's test against all annotated genes does not control
  for this. **Request.** Use a null that keeps gene length and window overlap (for example circularly shifted call
  sets or gene-length-matched resampling, as in the inversion GO test, Fig S21).
- **"Robust to background selection" (main.tex:313-327, 385).** Fold enrichment often rises when a gene set shrinks
  (291 calls against 623), so rising fold values do not show that the screen removed BGS-driven calls. **Request.**
  Compare the BGS-robust set with random subsets of the Tier-1 set of the same size, matched on coding density.
  Report the FDR of the photoreception terms in the full set (FDR 0.02-0.03 at fold 1.8-1.9, i.e. a weak signal) in
  the abstract wording.
- **The Z rows in Table 4.** Fig 5 shows *Pasilla* and *Esrrg* below the stratified 99th percentile. The Z calls get
  no corroboration and no BGS test, yet they appear in a table titled "High-confidence selective-sweep candidates"
  next to autosomal regions that passed both. "High-confidence" is also used for the 24 concordant regions
  (abstract) and for the 11 BGS-robust regions (main.tex:335). **Request.** Put the Z calls in a separate table or mark
  them as lower tier, and use one meaning of "high-confidence".
- **Tier 2 is described as a subset of Tier 1 but is not one.** Tier 2 is defined from stratified outliers
  (supplement.tex:186-187), and only 21 of 24 regions contain a Tier-1 centre (main.tex:299-300). The phrase
  "high-purity subset of the classifier calls" (main.tex:300-301) should be corrected. The Fig 5 header calls Tier 2
  "diploSHIC-HMM outlier + a footprint method", which disagrees with the supplement definition.

### M9. Z chromosome: achiasmy is inferred from inconsistent Z effective sizes and the uncertainty is not carried through

- **main.tex:401, supplement.tex:131, 164-175, Fig S12.** The LD analysis gives N_e,Z/N_e,A = 0.68-0.76 after
  correcting for the lower Z mutation rate. The ReLERNN Z network was trained with Ne × 0.60, which is the raw π ratio
  and includes the mutation-rate difference. With the corrected ~0.72, r_Z/r_A from ReLERNN falls from 1.20 to ~1.0,
  midway between the equal-rates (0.67) and achiasmy (1.33) predictions. **Request.** Use one N_e,Z throughout.
- **Female rate.** From the stated intervals (ρ_Z/ρ_A 0.88-1.28 from the decay fit and 0.78-1.67 from rescaling,
  N_e,Z/N_e,A 0.68-0.76), r_f/r_m ranges from below zero up to about 0.15-0.30. The data therefore show strongly
  reduced female recombination, not necessarily none. **Request.** Propagate the uncertainty (bootstrap across both
  ratios together), report a CI for r_f/r_m, and change "achiasmate" to "strongly reduced, consistent with
  achiasmy" in the abstract and Discussion, as the authors' comment at main.tex:49 and 401 already suggests.
- **Mutation bias.** "A female mutation rate two to four times the male rate" (main.tex:49, 401) does not match α =
  0.33 (95% CI 0.10-0.70), i.e. 1.4 to 10 times, and the upper CI reaches 1 under joint matching (Table S10). Report
  the interval.

  contribution?.]"), "Draft \today" in the title block, and the orange `\comment{}` notes listed above.
