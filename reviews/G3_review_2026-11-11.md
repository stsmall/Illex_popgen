# Mock G3 review: "Cryptic genomic variation in a panmictic squid"

Reviewed draft: `main.pdf` / `supplement.pdf` at commit `8a87bd4` (Draft November 11, 2026, 35 + 43 pages).
Locations are main-text **page:line** (the line numbers in the left margin of `main.pdf`) unless marked S (supplement
page). Tick the box when a comment is addressed. The reference check is in
[`G3_reference_check_2026-11-11.md`](G3_reference_check_2026-11-11.md).

## Summary of the submission

The authors re-analyse 633 whole genomes of *Illex illecebrosus* (sequenced by Baker et al. 2025) against a new
conspecific chromosome-level assembly. They confirm range-wide panmixia, discover a ~19 Mb inversion on chromosome 2
(2a derived, p = 0.626), date it with a barrier-aware coalescent simulator (msinv) using ABC-SMC model choice and
neural posterior estimation (NPE), fit a genome-wide expansion, test for multiple-merger coalescence, scan for sweeps
with a stratified diploS/HIC + RAiSD/SweepFinder2 pipeline, and infer achiasmate female meiosis from Z-linked LD.

## Overall assessment

The dataset is valuable and the inversion analysis is unusually thorough for a non-model marine species: the
polarisation from phased haplotype assemblies and two outgroups is careful, the NPE is calibrated and its joint
posterior is shown, and the authors are candid about the poor calibration of the sweep classifier. The paper is
suitable for G3 after **major revision**. The main problems are (1) internal numerical inconsistencies between text,
figures, tables and supplement, several of which a referee will find in minutes, (2) an unvalidated new simulator that
carries the central inversion result, (3) ABC model choice with no reported model-choice validation and posteriors
that pile against prior bounds, (4) several overstated conclusions, and (5) G3 format and data-availability gaps.
The manuscript also tries to do a great deal (inversion, demography, coalescent class, recombination, BGS, MK, sweeps,
Z achiasmy, anomalous blocks), and the sweep and Z sections read as a second paper.

## Major comments

### M1. The inversion result rests on a simulator with no validation in the paper
- [ ] **6:230, 18:779, S Table S1.** msinv is cited as software only ("Small, 2025"). It is new, unpublished, and its
  behaviour inside heterokaryotypes is exactly what determines the age, gene-flux and LD fits. A referee will ask for
  evidence that msinv reproduces a forward simulator (e.g. SLiM with an inversion and gene conversion) for the
  statistics used (within-arrangement π, dxy, SFS, JSFS, LD by distance) at the fitted parameters, or at least for
  the neutral case with a known barrier. Without it, "Model choice with a barrier-aware coalescent simulator" is not
  independently checkable. If the authors decide not to include a validation, the Limitations should say so
  explicitly and point to the msinv test suite.
- [ ] **19:830–831 and S Table S1.** The Data availability statement and Table S1 cite **msinv v0.3.5 (commit 35412bf)**.
  The analysis used msinv 0.4.1 (which changed crossover suppression in heterokaryotypes). This must be corrected to
  the version and commit that produced the results, or the reader will reproduce the wrong simulator.

### M2. ABC model choice needs validation, and several posteriors are prior-bounded
- [ ] **6:247–252.** Posterior model probabilities (0.997, 0.993) and the "59-fold" evidence come from ABC-SMC with 39
  non-sufficient summary statistics, 15 generations and 1,000 particles. ABC model choice with insufficient statistics
  can be badly miscalibrated (Robert et al. 2011 PNAS; Marin et al. 2014). Please report a model-choice confusion
  matrix (or posterior-probability calibration) on pseudo-observed data simulated under each model, and state whether
  the SMC had converged (tolerance trajectory, acceptance rate, effective sample size in the last generations).
- [ ] **6:252, 12:515–516.** "A start from a single copy carries 2% of the ABC-SMC posterior mass" is a model-choice
  statement inside one model class. Its sensitivity to the p0 prior (log-uniform 10⁻³–10⁻¹) should be shown, because
  the soft-start conclusion (Discussion section "The derived arrangement may not have started from a single copy")
  depends on it.
- [ ] **S14 (joint posterior) and 6:254–256, Table 2.** The marginal of log10 p0 piles up against its upper prior
  bound (−1, i.e. p0 = 0.1), and t_sel/t_inv piles against 1. The reported "founding frequency of about 0.05
  (0.004–0.10)" is therefore partly the prior. Say so, and either widen the p0 prior or present p0 as a lower bound in
  the same way as s.
- [ ] **6:258–260.** Same for s: the 95% lower bound (1.8 × 10⁻⁵) and the flat posterior above 10⁻⁴ are set by the
  prior range (10⁻⁶–10⁻¹). State the prior next to the bound.
- [ ] **6:241–244 and 18:787–791.** Model choice is by ABC-SMC but parameters are by NPE, and NPE was trained only on
  soft starts of model B. Explain why NPE was not used for model choice as well (or why the two-step approach is
  coherent), and confirm the NPE prior is identical to the ABC prior for model B.
- [ ] **7:267–268.** Without gene flux the barrier age (260 ka, 210–330) is much younger than with it (380 ka,
  250–820), and the posterior correlation of t_inv with the gene-flux rate is ρ = 0.65 (6:262). The age is therefore
  conditional on the gene-flux model. Discuss what this implies for the robustness of "380 ka", and report the
  no-flux posterior in Table 2 or the supplement.

### M3. Goodness of fit of the inversion model
- [ ] **7:268–274, S25.** The joint posterior predictive p = 0.26 is reported, but LD at 8–20 kb in 2+ fails
  (p = 0.01) and short-range LD in 2a is at the edge (S25 also shows LD bins 1–3 in 2a near the upper tail). LD is the
  statistic most sensitive to the recombination model inside heterokaryotypes. Discuss what this misfit means (wrong
  recombination rate inside arrangements, background selection, gene conversion tract length?) rather than only
  listing it.
- [ ] **7:283, 18:783–785.** Each simulation is 40 kb of inversion plus 40 kb of control, but the inversion is 19 Mb
  and gene flux, LD and divergence may vary along it (the authors note a 40% depletion of near-fixed differences in
  the outermost megabase, 7:295–296). Justify that a single 40-kb segment represents the whole inversion, or show the
  observed statistics are computed comparably (centre only? all windows averaged?).
- [ ] **6:246, 16:702–705, Table 3 vs 18:791–792.** The inversion baseline (chr2 flanks: N_anc 507k → N0 9.1M over
  383k generations, r = 5.5 × 10⁻¹⁰) differs from the genome-wide moments model (548k → 6.8M over 770k generations)
  and its r is ~4× below the ReLERNN map (0.24 cM/Mb ≈ 2.4 × 10⁻⁹). The barrier age (380 ka) is close to the onset of
  growth in the flank baseline (383k generations). A referee will ask whether the age estimate is anchored to the
  baseline's growth onset. Show sensitivity of t_inv to the baseline (e.g. rerun NPE posterior predictive or a small
  ABC under the genome-wide moments model).

### M4. Numerical inconsistencies between text, figures, tables and supplement
Each of these needs to be reconciled against the code output.
- [ ] **Sex counts.** Text and Fig. 2 caption: 342 ZZ males / 291 ZO females (4:140, 29 caption, Table 1). Fig. 2b
  legend: ZZ 345 / ZO 270 / unknown 6 (621). Fig. 2c legend: ZZ 346 / ZO 273 / unknown 10 (629, not 633). Z scan and Table S10: 330 ZZ
  males and 289 females (10:465–467). Explain each subset in the captions.
- [ ] **PCA sample size.** Fig. 2a/b and Fig. S1 use n = 621, text says 633. State why 12 are excluded.
- [ ] **Tier-1 counts.** 10:415–416 "1,651 raw calls to 677 windows (623 localized autosomal calls, 437 hard and 186
  soft)". 677 − 623 = 54 calls are unaccounted for. Fig. S13b shows 677. Fig. S18 gives 627 total (440 hard, 187
  soft). Abstract (1:22) 627. Make the arithmetic explicit (e.g. calls in chr2/masked blocks/non-localised).
- [ ] **Fig. 5 caption vs text.** Caption (32) says the highlighted points are "robust against background selection
  (25 windows across 24 regions)". Text (11:455) says only 11 of the 24 concordant regions pass the background-selection
  test, and Fig. 5's own header says "240 stratified outliers → 25 corroborated windows in 24 regions" with no BGS
  filter. Fix the caption or the figure.
- [ ] **Near-fixed differences.** 8:318–319 "3,513 near-fixed differences ... (Supplementary Fig. S5)" but Fig. S5a
  reports 2,229 near-fixed differences. Different sample sets (all homokaryotypes vs 40+40 for the 2D SFS) or
  thresholds? Say which, or cite the right figure.
- [ ] **Karyotype FST.** Table 1/2 0.365, 5:185 0.37, 5:188 "median of 0.34 (maximum 0.53)", Fig. 3b caption
  "median FST = 0.37 compared to 0.01 in collinear regions" vs 5:187 "median 0.006". Use one estimator and one
  summary.
- [ ] **Per-SNP FST estimator.** Results (4:160, S2, S19) say per-SNP **Hudson** FST (Bhatia et al.). Methods
  (18:770–771) say per-SNP geographic FST was computed with plink2 using **Weir–Cockerham**. One of them is wrong.
- [ ] **SNP count.** 4:165 "20,117,984 SNPs" vs Fig. S19 "20,276,930 SNPs".
- [ ] **Effective size.** Abstract "approximately seven million" (moments 6.8M), 8:339 "Stairway Plot 2 and GONE2
  concur on ... Ne in the 1.5–1.7 × 10⁷ range", but Fig. 4a shows GONE2 near 5 × 10⁶, the momentsLD point at
  ~1.5 × 10⁶, and the inversion baseline uses 9.1 × 10⁶. 8:344–345 and 15:645–646 claim SFS and LD contemporary Ne
  "agree", but 6.8 × 10⁶ vs 1.5 × 10⁶ is a 4.5-fold difference. Either reconcile or soften.
- [ ] **Fig. 4a Stairway dip.** Stairway Plot 2 shows a ~20-fold bottleneck around 1–3 × 10³ generations ago that is
  never mentioned. Address it (artefact of the recent-time resolution, or real?).
- [ ] **Fig. 3c scale.** π and dxy in Fig. 3c are ~1–4 × 10⁻³, but the text gives π = 0.0063 for the same flanks
  (6:246) and 0.0093 genome-wide. State the estimator and denominator (called genotypes over all accessible sites vs
  ANGSD genotype likelihoods) in the caption, or the figure will look wrong to readers.
- [ ] **Tajima's D.** Table 1 −2.07 genome-wide, 8:338 forward-simulated −1.92, Fig. 6b per-division medians around
  −1.5. The per-division values are for n = 25 subsamples, so D is not comparable. Say so in the caption.
- [ ] **Fig. 6c.** All pairwise FST values are negative (−0.017 to −0.004). Explain the estimator bias, and the claim
  that "the clustering follows sample size" should be supported or removed.
- [ ] **Odds ratio.** 10:421 "odds ratio 242", consistent with S13. Keep consistent if numbers change.

### M5. Overstated conclusions
- [ ] **4:172.** "there is no geographic local adaptation anywhere in the genome" is an absence-of-evidence statement
  from 20 permutations of division labels. With 20 permutations the permutation maximum and p-values have coarse
  resolution, and no power analysis is given. Rephrase ("we find no evidence of ...") and report how many
  permutations were run and the detectable effect size.
- [ ] **29 (Fig. 1 caption).** "indicating that the inversion is not locally adaptive" does not follow from a
  non-clinal frequency along latitude. Local adaptation along depth, temperature or season (sampling 2022–2023) is
  not excluded. Soften.
- [ ] **8:334–335** "an unambiguous, genome-wide signature of expansion" conflicts with 15:640–641, where the authors
  correctly say negative D cannot by itself distinguish growth from sweepstakes or linked selection. Remove
  "unambiguous".
- [ ] **7:288–290.** Sentence fragment and overclaim ("Concluding that it is not locally adaptive by geography ...
  cannot apply").
- [ ] **12–13, Discussion "Methodological lessons".** Claims that msinv "resolves ... the long phase at a balanced
  equilibrium" should be tempered given M1–M3.
- [ ] **1:25–28 and 15–16:663–683.** The achiasmy inference chains ρZ/ρA (CI 0.88–1.28), α (CI 0.10–0.70) and a Z
  effective size of 0.68–0.76. Propagate these uncertainties into the female recombination estimate and give a CI
  rather than "near zero". The abstract states the conclusion without that uncertainty.

### M6. Sweep scan
- [ ] **9–10:397–403.** The classifier assigns 1% of windows to neutral and 91% to linked classes. After this, the
  stratified empirical-outlier ranking makes Tier-1 an outlier list by construction (a fixed fraction of each stratum).
  State the expected false-discovery rate, or at least how many Tier-1 calls would be expected under a null where
  the genome has no sweeps (e.g. apply the full pipeline to neutral simulations with added per-window θ
  heterogeneity matching the empirical windows).
- [ ] **10–11:426–449.** GO enrichment of genes under sweep calls is sensitive to gene length and gene clustering
  (long neural genes span more windows). Use a length-aware or window-permutation null (e.g. circular permutation of
  calls along the genome) rather than Fisher's exact test against the annotated background.
- [ ] **10:427–428.** "One-third of calls fall in gene-poor, often transposon-rich windows and are excluded from
  enrichment analyses." This exclusion criterion should be defined in Methods.
- [ ] **11:455–457.** "fine-scale localization confirms all 11 as recent sweeps by the sign of Tajima's D at the
  diversity valley". Negative D is ubiquitous in this genome (D ≈ −2 everywhere), so the sign of D cannot confirm a
  sweep. Use a contrast against the local or stratum background.
