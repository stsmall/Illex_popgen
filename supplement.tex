\section*{Supplementary methods}

\paragraph{Read processing and variant calling.}
Raw reads for all 633 individuals were downloaded from ENA project PRJEB101095 and processed with grenepipe
\citep{czech2022grenepipe}. Reads were trimmed with fastp \citep{chen2018fastp} and mapped to the
\texttt{Illex\_F24} primary assembly with BWA-MEM2 \citep{vasimuddin2019bwamem2}, and duplicates were removed with
Picard MarkDuplicates. Each individual was genotyped with GATK HaplotypeCaller in gVCF mode, and the gVCFs were
jointly genotyped with all sites retained \citep{poplin2018gatk}. Variant records were annotated with snpEff and
soft-filtered with bcftools \citep{danecek2021samtools}. A site was removed if its quality was below 20, its
root-mean-square mapping quality was below 40, or its strand-bias score (FS) exceeded 60. It was also removed if
it was missing in half or more of the individuals, if it lay in a GC-corrected high-depth window identified with
mosdepth, or if it lay outside the accessibility mask. Sites at intermediate allele frequency (0.1 to 0.9) with a
strong excess of heterozygotes (ExcHet $<0.001$) were flagged as likely paralogous and removed. Only sites passing
every filter were used. The accessibility mask is the 1.43~Gb of the 3.47-Gb assembly that lies outside repeats annotated with Earl~Grey. Mappability was computed with GenMap 1.3.0 \citep{pockrandt2020genmap} for 150-mers with up to two mismatches, matching the 151-bp reads. Across the assembly 14.7\% of positions are not uniquely mappable, but within the accessibility mask only 3.8\% (53.7~Mb) are, because the repeat annotation already removes nearly all multi-copy sequence. Non-unique sequence is concentrated six-fold in the 22 anomalous-diversity blocks (23\% of their accessible sequence), and outside them it is 2.7\% of accessible sequence. For the sweep scan and the gene-set analyses, the mask was extended by the 22 anomalous-diversity blocks (129~Mb, Supplementary Table~S9), in which collapsed duplications and sequence duplicated within the assembly make genotype-based statistics unreliable.

\paragraph{Diversity and demography.}
All site-frequency spectra and diversity statistics were computed with ANGSD 0.940 \citep{korneliussen2014angsd}
directly from the deduplicated BAM alignments of the 633 individuals, not from the called genotypes, so that the
uncertainty of genotypes at about 9-fold depth is carried through as genotype likelihoods. Each chromosome was
run separately with the options \texttt{-doSaf 1 -doCounts 1 -GL 1 -minQ 20 -minMapQ 20 -remove\_bads 1
-only\_proper\_pairs 1 -minInd 1 -setMinDepthInd 1}, restricted with \texttt{-sites} to the accessibility mask and
with the reference supplied as both \texttt{-ref} and \texttt{-anc}. \texttt{-GL 1} is the SAMtools
genotype-likelihood model, and because the reference is not an ancestral sequence all spectra are folded. The
folded spectrum was estimated with \texttt{realSFS -fold 1 -tole 1e-8 -maxIter 200}, per-site thetas with
\texttt{realSFS saf2theta -fold 1}, and $\pi$, Watterson's $\theta$ and Tajima's $D$ with \texttt{thetaStat
do\_stat}, per chromosome and in 50-kb windows (10-kb step genome-wide, 50-kb step per division). The genome-wide
folded spectrum pooled across chromosomes is the input to the demographic models. Per-division statistics used the same options on at most 25 individuals per division (all individuals in 3O, 4S and 4T), and the Z was run in the 330
confidently sexed males. Per-karyotype spectra and genotype-likelihood $\Fst$ between arrangements used the AA and
BB homozygotes as separate sample sets. Stairway~Plot~2, moments (a two-epoch growth model fitted to the folded
spectrum, with Godambe confidence intervals), and GONE2 were fitted as described in Supplementary Table~S1, and
the moments model was checked by forward simulation, which reproduced the observed $\pi$ and Tajima's $D$.

\paragraph{Genotype-based statistics.}
Statistics that need individual genotypes were computed with pg\_gpu \citep{pggpu} from the filtered VCF.
Windowed $\pi$, $\dxy$ and Hudson $\Fst$ between arrangements used biallelic SNPs in 100-kb windows, with the AA
($n=254$) and BB ($n=95$) homozygotes as the two populations and missing genotypes included pairwise. The between-division $\Fst$ matrix (Hudson), the linkage-disequilibrium triangles (genotype $r^2$ among common SNPs), and the principal-component analyses were computed the same way. The per-SNP geographic $\Fst$ scans (Supplementary Figs.~S2, S19) used plink2 \citep{chang2015plink2} on every biallelic SNP with a minor-allele frequency above 0.05 in the pooled 632 individuals from the ten divisions with more than one sample (division 3N, with a single individual, was excluded), giving 20{,}276{,}930 SNPs. Per-division allele counts were converted to Hudson $\Fst$ for each of the 45 division pairs \citep{bhatia2013}, at sites genotyped in at least 40 individuals in that pair and with at least two alleles in each division. The per-SNP value is the ratio of averages over informative pairs (the sum of the numerators over the sum of the denominators), without clipping, keeping sites informative in at least 20 of the 45 pairs. The null was built by shuffling division labels among the 632 individuals 20 times (division sizes kept) and recomputing $\Fst$ at the same SNPs in the same way. Thresholds are quantiles of the pooled null over the autosomes outside the chromosome-2 inversion ($3.996\times10^{8}$ values), and the expected count above each threshold is the number of SNPs times the null tail rate.

\paragraph{The inversion.}
Karyotypes were called from the first principal component of a local PCA across chromosome~2 with a
three-component Gaussian mixture (95.3\% of individuals assigned with posterior probability above 0.95).
Breakpoints were placed at the step change in between-arrangement differentiation and confirmed by a strand flip
in the AnchorWave alignment to \coin{}. Arrangement polarity was inferred only from diagnostic sites at which
\coin{} carries the \illex{} alternate allele, because sites at which it matches the reference are circular when
the reference is itself inverted. The age and frequency trajectory were fitted with msinv as described in the next paragraphs. Unannotated, non-repeat sequence in the inversion was searched with DIAMOND \texttt{blastx} against seven cephalopod proteomes, with transposable elements from Earl~Grey \citep{baril2024earlgrey} and transcripts from StringTie2 \citep{kovaka2019stringtie} as supporting evidence. This recovered 14 likely protein-coding loci that the annotation lacks. They are provided as \texttt{protein\_match} features in version 3 of the annotation and are not counted among the 89 gene models of the inversion. Fixed differences were sites with an allele-frequency difference of at least 0.9
between AA and BB homozygotes. Gene-ontology enrichment of the inversion genes used Fisher's exact test against
the annotated-gene background in all three ontologies, compared with 500 random gene sets of equal size, and gene
density was compared with all autosomal windows of the same length (0.5-Mb steps). The two drift bounds are not separate estimates. They are analytic diffusion calculations that take the peak frequency (0.70--0.74) and the duration of the fall from the msinv grid-search fit and $\Ne$ from the fitted demography, and ask whether drift alone could produce that trajectory. The attainment bound used the
expected time for a new neutral mutation to reach frequency $x$, conditional on reaching it ($1.936\,\Ne$ at $x=0.70$ and $2.107\,\Ne$ at $x=0.74$), and the decline bound used the drift standard deviation $\sqrt{p(1-p)\,t/(2\Ne)}$ with $\Ne$ from the
fitted demography over the decline. The load test compared the zero-fold fraction among the most differentiated
coding sites (genotype-likelihood $\Fst$, 40 individuals per karyotype) with all coding sites in the inversion by
Fisher's exact test, and per-transcript $\dxy$ was normalised to its 100-kb window and compared with collinear
control transcripts.

\paragraph{Inversion coalescent model.}
The age and frequency history of the inversion were fitted with msinv v0.3.5 (\url{https://github.com/stsmall/msinv}, commit \texttt{35412bf}), a coalescent simulator with recombination that writes tree sequences. It uses the sequentially Markov approximation \citep{mcvean2005smc}, extended to two karyotype classes as in \citet{peischl2013}, in which two lineages coalesce only where they share ancestral material. This approximation reproduces the expected coalescence time at each site, which is all the fitted statistics use. The model code, fitting scripts, and every result quoted here are in the \texttt{illex/} and \texttt{results/illex/} directories of that repository. Inside the inversion every lineage carries a karyotype label, standard (S, the AA arrangement) or inverted (I, the BB arrangement). Two lineages of the same class coalesce at rate $1/[2N(t)\,p_c(t)]$ per generation, where $N(t)$ is the effective size and $p_c(t)$ the frequency of their class, and recombination within the inversion occurs only between chromosomes of the same arrangement, at rate $r\,p_c(t)$. Lineages of different classes cannot coalesce while the barrier exists. msinv can also exchange short tracts between arrangements by gene flux, with the position-dependent rate of \citet{peischl2013}, which rises from zero at the breakpoints toward the centre of the inversion. In these fits the flux rate was set to $10^{-15}$ per site per generation, which is effectively zero, because divergence between arrangements is flat from the breakpoints to the centre of the inversion (edge-to-centre ratio of $\dxy/\pi$ 0.999). Backward in time, the barrier is removed at $t_\mathrm{inv}$, when the inverted lineages rejoin the standard class. $t_\mathrm{inv}$ is therefore the age of the recombination barrier, and the model does not impose a single-copy origin or a separate rise of the new arrangement.

Each simulation was a 37{,}148-bp sequence ($4N_0rL=2000$), consisting of a 29{,}718-bp inversion with 3{,}715~bp of collinear sequence on each side, and sampled 100 chromosomes of each arrangement. The demography was the fitted moments model, with exponential growth from 547{,}928 to 6{,}808{,}096 over the last 769{,}519 generations and a constant size before that. The recombination rate was $r=1.977\times10^{-9}$ per site per generation, the rate of the collinear part of chromosome~2 in the recombination map, and $\mu=3\times10^{-9}$. Statistics were computed from branch lengths within the inversion, so the fitted ratios do not depend on $\mu$, and $\mu$ sets only the absolute time scale through the demography.

\paragraph{Frequency trajectory.}
The frequency of the inverted arrangement followed a deterministic trajectory, with neutrality within each arrangement. Going back from the present, the frequency is $p_\mathrm{now}=0.374$ until $t_\mathrm{decline}$, the time at which the fall ended. Before that it falls from a plateau $p_\mathrm{hist}$ over a duration $t_\mathrm{fall}$, and it stays at $p_\mathrm{hist}$ from the start of the fall back to $t_\mathrm{inv}$. Forward in time, the fall follows $dp/dt=(s/p^{*})\,p(1-p)(p^{*}-p)$ toward the equilibrium $p^{*}=p_\mathrm{now}$, the form expected under overdominance, with $s$ solved so that the frequency comes within 0.01\% of $p_\mathrm{now}$ in exactly $t_\mathrm{fall}$. Most of the fall therefore happens early, and for a 100-ky fall from 0.74 the frequency reaches 0.40 within the first 28~ky. The value of $s$ summarises the shape of the fitted trajectory. It is not a fitted selective mechanism.

\paragraph{Fitted statistics.}
Three statistics were fitted. The diversity ratio $\pi_I/\pi_S$ and the divergence ratio $\dxy/\pi_I$ were computed for each replicate and averaged over replicates. Their empirical values, $1.356\pm0.048$ and $1.385\pm0.021$, come from 254 AA and 95 BB individuals over the inversion (189 windows of 100~kb between 60.5 and 79.5~Mb, pg\_gpu), with standard errors from a delete-one jackknife over 1-Mb blocks. The third statistic is the ratio between arrangements of the singleton-class fraction of the site-frequency spectrum, which in the simulations is the first bin of the folded per-arrangement spectrum projected to 20 chromosomes. Its empirical value from per-arrangement ANGSD spectra is $0.8256\pm0.0076$ in the inversion and $1.0161$ in a collinear control region (chr2:10--30~Mb), and the fitted target was the control-calibrated ratio $0.8125\pm0.0076$.

\paragraph{Fitting and profiles.}
The trajectory was fitted by grid search. Coarse exploratory grids spanned $t_\mathrm{inv}$ from 200~ky to 6~My and $p_\mathrm{hist}$ from 0.45 to 0.88. The final grid covered $t_\mathrm{inv}$ of 700, 750, 800, 850 and 900~ky, $p_\mathrm{hist}$ of 0.70, 0.74, 0.78 and 0.82 (0.70 and 0.74 only at 700 and 750~ky), $t_\mathrm{decline}$ of 100, 175 and 250~ka, and $t_\mathrm{fall}$ of 50, 100 and 200~ky, with 48 replicates per cell. Two further grids around the best cells extended $t_\mathrm{decline}$ to the present (0, 25 and 50~ka) and $t_\mathrm{fall}$ to 400 and 600~ky. Cells were scored with a weighted $\chi^{2}=\sum_k[(\hat{x}_k-x_k)/\mathrm{SE}_k]^{2}$ over the three statistics, where $\hat{x}_k$ is the simulated mean, $x_k$ the empirical target, and $\mathrm{SE}_k$ its measurement standard error. Adding the simulation variance of each cell to the denominator changes $\chi^{2}$ by less than 0.1 and leaves every profile unchanged. The best cells lie at $t_\mathrm{inv}$ of 800--850~ky and $p_\mathrm{hist}=0.74$, with a fall of 50--100~ky that ended 175--250~ka ($\chi^{2}$ of 2.6--2.9), and the differences among them are within simulation noise. Profiles of $\Delta\chi^{2}$, in which the other parameters were re-optimised over the grid, bound each parameter (Supplementary Fig.~S14). The barrier age is 800--850~ky, with $\Delta\chi^{2}$ of 10 at 750~ky, 35 at 700~ky and 11 at 900~ky. The plateau is 0.70--0.74, with $\Delta\chi^{2}$ of 0.5 at 0.70, 24 at 0.78 and 90 at 0.82. Fall durations of 50 to 200~ky are not excluded ($\Delta\chi^{2}<1$), while 400 and 600~ky give $\Delta\chi^{2}$ of 8.4 and 81.5. The fall ended at least $\sim$100~ka ago. Ends at 100, 175 and 250~ka are equally supported, 250~ka being the oldest end tested, while an end at 50~ka gives $\Delta\chi^{2}=3.2$ and an end at the present gives 35.5. Across the fall durations that are not excluded the trajectory implies $s$ from $7\times10^{-5}$ to $3\times10^{-4}$ ($\Ne s$ of about 200 to 1{,}000 at the middle of the fall), and $s\approx1.5\times10^{-4}$ ($\Ne s\approx470$) for a 100-ky fall. The $\Delta\chi^{2}$ thresholds were calibrated by a parametric bootstrap (\texttt{illex/scripts/param\_bootstrap.py} in the msinv repository at the same commit). The grid was rebuilt with per-replicate output (202 cells of 48 replicates), which reproduces the profiles above within simulation noise. At the best cell (850~ky, 0.74, a 100-ky fall ending 175~ka), 2{,}000 pseudo-observed datasets were drawn by adding noise with the jackknife standard errors and the observed correlation between $\pi_I/\pi_S$ and $\dxy/\pi_I$ ($-0.71$) to the simulated statistics. The statistics of every cell were recomputed from a resample of its own replicates, and each pseudo-dataset was refitted on the same grid with the same $\chi^{2}$. The 95th percentile of the profile $\Delta\chi^{2}$ at the true value is 1.8 for the barrier age, 4.6 for the plateau, 2.0 for the end of the fall and 3.0 for its duration, and the grid values within these calibrated thresholds are exactly the ranges given above. The bootstrap holds the demography, mutation rate and recombination rate at their fitted values, so these are 95\% intervals conditional on the model.

\paragraph{Simulated length and validation.}
The fits used a 29.7-kb simulated inversion, and the fitted values were applied to the 19-Mb inversion. At the best-fitting trajectory, the three fitted statistics are nearly constant over a 25-fold range of simulated lengths (3.0 to 74~kb of inversion, 120 replicates per length). Extrapolating the 95\% confidence limits of their slopes from the fitted length to 19~Mb bounds the change at 4.6\% for $\pi_I/\pi_S$, 1.7\% for $\dxy/\pi_I$ and 2.8\% for the singleton ratio. With an inversion, under constant population size and without gene flux, the mean pairwise coalescence times within each arrangement, between arrangements, and in the collinear flanks match their exact expectations for inverted-arrangement frequencies of 0.2 to 0.74 and barrier ages of $0.25N$ to $4N$ generations (24 comparisons, 200 replicates each, all within 2.7 standard errors). The current version of msinv (commit \texttt{e53f0c6}) also agrees with forward simulations in SLiM \citep{haller2019slim} that share each replicate's realised frequency trajectory, in four scenarios covering a short and a long barrier, low recombination, and an arrangement held near 0.74 by overdominance (200 replicates each). Across these scenarios the mean coalescence times agree within 4\%, the medians of both fitted ratios agree within 4\%, and $|d|<0.2$ for every statistic. At the best-fitting trajectory the fitted statistics from this version and from v0.3.5 differ by at most 0.3\%. The fits use only the means of the fitted statistics, which at the fitted length vary by about 2\% among replicates. Statistics that depend on the correlation of genealogies along the sequence are not used.

\paragraph{Recombination map.}
ReLERNN \citep{adrion2020relernn} was run in unphased mode. Training data were 12{,}000 coalescent simulations
(with 1{,}000 each for validation and testing) under the Stairway~Plot~2 demography with $\mu=3\times10^{-9}$,
masked by the inaccessible regions with a window-level masking threshold of 0.9. The network was trained for
50 epochs and predictions were corrected with 20 bootstrap replicates in each of 20 rate slices. Individuals with
no genotype calls on a chromosome cannot enter the unphased model, so one of the 330 confidently sexed males was
removed and 329 were used. Chromosomes~2 and~42 were predicted with the same network, and the Z was estimated
separately. The map pools all karyotypes and reads linkage disequilibrium within windows of about 19~kb, so it
does not resolve the barrier between chromosome-2 arrangements, which is shown instead by long-range linkage
disequilibrium (Supplementary Figs.~S4, S24).

\paragraph{Z recombination and sex-biased mutation.}
Linkage disequilibrium on the Z and on the autosomes was compared in the 330 ZZ males, the only individuals that
carry two Z copies. Two-locus statistics ($D^2$ and $\pi_2$, unbiased for unphased genotypes
\citep{ragsdale2020unphased}) were computed with pg\_gpu \citep{pggpu} on accessible biallelic SNPs in 2-Mb windows,
86 on the autosomes (at 10 and 40~Mb of each autosome, excluding the chromosome-2 inversion and the
anomalous-diversity blocks) and nine spaced every 4~Mb along the Z, with SNP pairs binned by distance from 0 to
5~kb. The decay of $\sigma_D^2=D^2/\pi_2$ with distance was fitted to the expectation of \citet{ohta1971} plus a
constant sample-size term, giving the population-scaled rate $\rho=4N_e r$ per base for each chromosome class. The
ratio $\rho_Z/\rho_A$ was also estimated without a model, as the distance rescaling that best superimposes the two
decay curves. Confidence intervals come from 200 bootstrap resamples of windows. Because the Z recombines only in
males and spends two thirds of its time in them, $\rho_Z/\rho_A=(N_{e,Z}/N_{e,A})\,(4/3)\,r_m/(r_m+r_f)$, where
$r_m$ and $r_f$ are the male and female per-generation rates and the male rate is taken to be the same on the Z
and the autosomes. With $N_{e,Z}/N_{e,A}=3/4$, equal recombination in the two sexes predicts $\rho_Z/\rho_A=0.5$
and achiasmate female meiosis predicts 1 \citep{coffing2026}.

Sex-biased mutation was estimated from synonymous divergence between \illex{} and \textit{Sthenoteuthis
oualaniensis} \citep{li2022flyingsquid}, a member of the same family whose chromosome~46 is syntenic with the \illex{} Z \citep{small2026}. The \illex{} annotation was transferred to the
\textit{S.~oualaniensis} assembly with LiftOn \citep{chao2024lifton}, one transcript per gene was aligned codon by
codon with MACSE 2.07 \citep{ranwez2018macse}, which allows frameshifts in the transferred sequence, and codons
with gaps, frameshifts, ambiguous bases or stop codons were removed. Synonymous and nonsynonymous divergence
($d_S$, $d_N$) were computed per gene by the method of \citet{nei1986} with a Jukes--Cantor correction. Genes in
the chromosome-2 inversion or the anomalous-diversity blocks were excluded, leaving 8{,}649 autosomal and 109
Z-linked genes with $d_S>0$. The male-to-female mutation ratio $\alpha=\mu_m/\mu_f$ was obtained from the ratio
$R$ of mean Z to mean autosomal $d_S$ as $\alpha=(3R-2)/(4-3R)$ \citep{miyata1987}, as in \citet{coffing2025} and
\citet{coffing2026}. Z-linked genes carry more gene-body CpG methylation than autosomal genes in \illex{}, most strongly in females \citep{small2026}. If this extends to the germline, methylation-associated mutation raises the Z rate and biases $\alpha$ upward, so the female bias is if anything underestimated. To separate mutation from selective constraint and base composition, $R$ was recomputed with
autosomal genes matched to the Z genes by deciles of $d_N/d_S$ and of third-position GC content (GC3), and with
genes restricted to those above the autosomal median $d_N/d_S$. Confidence intervals come from 2{,}000 bootstrap
resamples of genes within strata.

Linkage disequilibrium decays at the same rate on the Z as on the autosomes, $\rho_Z/\rho_A=1.04$ (95\% CI
0.88--1.28) from the decay fit and 1.08 (0.78--1.67) from curve rescaling, with $\rho_A=0.026$ per base
(Supplementary Table~S10). Synonymous divergence is lower on the Z ($d_S=0.298$ vs 0.358, Mann--Whitney $p=0.009$),
giving $R=0.83$ and $\alpha=0.33$ (95\% CI 0.10--0.70), a female mutation rate about three times the male rate.
Z-linked genes are slightly more constrained (median $d_N/d_S$ 0.082 vs 0.113, $p=0.048$) and lower in GC3 (0.36
vs 0.42, $p<10^{-16}$). Matching on $d_N/d_S$ leaves $\alpha$ unchanged (0.36), and matching on GC3 raises it to
0.43, or 0.49 when combined with $d_N/d_S$, so the female bias holds under each control, with the upper
confidence limit reaching 1 only under joint matching. Because $R$ estimates $\mu_Z/\mu_A$, the ratio of male Z
to autosomal diversity ($\pi_Z/\pi_A=0.00563/0.0093=0.61$) implies $N_{e,Z}/N_{e,A}=0.68$--0.76 across these
estimates, close to the 3/4 expected under an equal breeding sex ratio. Combined with the
linkage-disequilibrium ratio, this gives $r_m/(r_m+r_f)\approx1.02$--1.14, consistent with achiasmate female
meiosis \citep{coffing2026} and inconsistent with equal recombination in the two sexes, which predicts 0.5.

\paragraph{Selective-sweep scan.}
diploS/HIC used 1.1-Mb feature windows of eleven 100-kb sub-windows and twelve diploid statistics per
sub-window. It was trained on hard and soft sweeps simulated in SLiM~5.2 and recapitated with msprime, and on
neutral msprime simulations, all under the fitted growth demography, at the empirical sample size, and with the
empirical per-individual missing-data structure. The final model classified held-out simulations with 81\%
accuracy (Supplementary Fig.~S7). Applied to the genome it assigns 1\% of windows to the neutral class and 91\% to
the linked classes, because real neutral windows carry three to eight times more spatial heterogeneity in
diversity among sub-windows than the simulations. We therefore used it as an outlier detector. The sweep score
$p_\mathrm{hard}+p_\mathrm{soft}$ was ranked within strata of callable fraction (quintiles) and coding density
(zero or not), and windows in the top 1\% of their stratum were outliers. RAiSD and SweepFinder2 statistics were
ranked the same way, and a region was concordant when a diploS/HIC outlier was supported by either. Tier-1 calls
came from a hidden Markov model with neutral, linked and sweep-centre states, whose emissions are the
prior-corrected class posteriors and whose dwell lengths follow the $\sim$400-kb sweep footprint scaled by the
recombination map. Calibrating this decoder by simulation proved infeasible, because at this population size the
simulated ancestral recombination graphs carry about $3.4\times10^{5}$ marginal trees per 2.5~Mb. Its settings
are therefore design-based and were checked against the concordant regions (Supplementary Fig.~S13). The background-selection screen compared each call's $\pi$, from the ANGSD windows it overlaps, with the value predicted from the fraction of coding sites within 1~Mb of its midpoint, $\hat\pi=0.010593-0.165473\,f_\mathrm{CDS}$, the genome-wide linear relationship between the two. A call was retained when $\pi-\hat\pi\leq-0.003$, i.e.\ when its diversity is at least 0.003 below the background-selection expectation. Retained calls with $f_\mathrm{CDS}$ above the genome median are those well below the expectation, and those below it are low-diversity calls in gene-poor sequence where background selection is weak.
Gene-ontology enrichment used genes with EnTAP \citep{hart2020entap} annotations that overlap a call, against all
annotated genes outside the Z and the masked blocks.

\paragraph{Anomalous-diversity blocks.}
Blocks were called where the rolling median of 10-kb $\pi$ exceeded twice the chromosome median over at least
1~Mb. Each was characterised by per-division $\pi$, between-division $\Fst$, local PCA, and genotype linkage
disequilibrium, and by read depth relative to 5-Mb flanks in all 633 individuals (samtools bedcov at mapping
quality 0 and 20). Long-read depth and heterozygous-site allele fractions came from PacBio HiFi reads of the
assembly individual and a second individual aligned to the assembly, and genotype-likelihood $\pi$ was
recomputed at mapping quality 60. Blocks with population depth at least 1.15 times their flanks were classed as
collapsed duplications and those at 0.90 or less as under-mapped, and the fraction of each block's accessible sequence that is not uniquely mappable was taken from GenMap (Supplementary Table~S9).
