\section*{Supplementary methods}

\paragraph{Read processing and variant calling.}
Raw reads for all 633 individuals were downloaded from ENA project PRJEB101095 and processed with grenepipe
\citep{czech2022grenepipe}. Twelve alignments hold almost no reads (93 to 2{,}009{,}803 autosomal reads in
\texttt{samtools idxstats}, against a median of 57{,}182{,}056, listed in \texttt{data/qc/empty\_bams\_12.tsv}), eleven
from division 4X and one from 3K. They were excluded from every count and estimate. Analyses that were run on all 633
received only missing genotypes or no reads from them, so those results rest on the remaining 621. Two further individuals, SQ23HBB023 (2+/2+, division 6C) and SQ23HBB124 (2a/2a, 6A), were excluded afterwards as likely contaminated (\texttt{data/qc/excluded\_14.tsv}). The two-component Gaussian mixture on the Z:autosome coverage ratio had called both female, because its female component is about four times broader than the male one (standard deviation 0.146 against 0.034) and so takes in ratios beyond the upper end of the male cluster. Both carry Z heterozygosity in the male range (0.0096 and 0.0036, against a female 95th percentile of 0.0014), Z coverage above every male (1.46 and 1.61, against a male maximum of 1.34), and the two most extreme scores on the second autosomal principal component. Counts, karyotype frequencies, all principal-component analyses, the karyotype-by-division and capture-depth tests are for the remaining 619. Genome-wide analyses run before the exclusion, namely the spectra, demography, sweep scans, recombination maps, geographic $\Fst$ scans and the anomalous-diversity screen, include the two (2 of 621 individuals). Of the inversion analyses, only the pooled linkage-disequilibrium map (\cref{sfig:ld}) includes them. The arrangement statistics, fixed differences and the inversion model fits use the karyotype groups of the final 619 (223 2a/2a and 97 2+/2+ for the between-arrangement comparisons). Reads were trimmed with fastp \citep{chen2018fastp} and mapped to the
\texttt{Illex\_F24} primary assembly with BWA-MEM2 \citep{vasimuddin2019bwamem2}, and duplicates were removed with
Picard MarkDuplicates. Each individual was genotyped with GATK HaplotypeCaller in gVCF mode, and the gVCFs were
jointly genotyped with all sites retained \citep{poplin2018gatk}. Variant records were annotated with snpEff and
soft-filtered with bcftools \citep{danecek2021samtools}. A site was removed if its quality was below 20, its
root-mean-square mapping quality was below 40, or its strand-bias score (FS) exceeded 60. It was also removed if
it was missing in half or more of the individuals, if it lay in a GC-corrected high-depth window identified with
mosdepth, or if it lay outside the accessibility mask. Sites at intermediate allele frequency (0.1 to 0.9) with a
strong excess of heterozygotes (ExcHet $<0.001$) were flagged as likely paralogous and removed. Only sites passing
every filter were used. The accessibility mask is the 1.43~Gb of the 3.47-Gb assembly that lies outside repeats annotated with Earl~Grey. Mappability was computed with GenMap 1.3.0 \citep{pockrandt2020genmap} for 150-mers with up to two mismatches, matching the 151-bp reads. Across the assembly 14.7\% of positions are not uniquely mappable, but within the accessibility mask only 3.8\% (53.7~Mb) are, because the repeat annotation already removes nearly all multi-copy sequence. Non-unique sequence is concentrated six-fold in the 22 anomalous-diversity blocks (23\% of their accessible sequence), and outside them it is 2.7\% of accessible sequence. For the sweep scan and the gene-set analyses, the mask was extended by the 22 anomalous-diversity blocks (129~Mb, Supplementary Table~S5), in which collapsed duplications and sequence duplicated within the assembly make genotype-based statistics unreliable.

\paragraph{Diversity and demography.}
All site-frequency spectra and diversity statistics were computed with ANGSD 0.940 \citep{korneliussen2014angsd}
directly from the deduplicated BAM alignments of the 621 individuals with data, not from the called genotypes, so that the
uncertainty of genotypes at about 2.5-fold depth (a median of 57.9 million autosomal reads of 150~bp per individual) is carried through as genotype likelihoods. Each chromosome was
run separately with the options \texttt{-doSaf 1 -doCounts 1 -GL 1 -minQ 20 -minMapQ 20 -remove\_bads 1
-only\_proper\_pairs 1 -minInd 1 -setMinDepthInd 1}, restricted with \texttt{-sites} to the accessibility mask and
with the reference supplied as both \texttt{-ref} and \texttt{-anc}. \texttt{-GL 1} is the SAMtools
genotype-likelihood model, and because the reference is not an ancestral sequence all spectra are folded. The
folded spectrum was estimated with \texttt{realSFS -fold 1 -tole 1e-8 -maxIter 200}, per-site thetas with
\texttt{realSFS saf2theta -fold 1}, and $\pi$, Watterson's $\theta$ and Tajima's $D$ with \texttt{thetaStat
do\_stat}, per chromosome and in 50-kb windows (10-kb step genome-wide, 50-kb step per division). The genome-wide
folded spectrum pooled across chromosomes is the input to the demographic models. Per-division statistics used the same options on at most 25 individuals per division (all individuals in 3O, 4S and 4T), and the Z was run in the 329
confidently sexed males with data. Per-karyotype spectra and genotype-likelihood $\Fst$ between arrangements used the 2a/2a and
2+/2+ homozygotes as separate sample sets. Stairway~Plot~2, moments (a two-epoch growth model fitted to the folded
spectrum, with Godambe confidence intervals), and GONE2 were fitted as described in Supplementary Table~S10, and
the moments model was checked by forward simulation, which reproduced the observed $\pi$ and Tajima's $D$.

The coalescent class was tested by comparing the normalised folded genome-wide SFS, projected to 40 chromosomes, with expected shapes simulated in msprime (100 replicates of 2~Mb, branch-length spectra) under a constant-size Kingman coalescent, Kingman with the fitted growth history, and Beta$(2-\alpha,\alpha)$ coalescents without growth for $\alpha$ of 1.05 to 1.99 (\texttt{beta\_vs\_kingman.py} in \texttt{pipeline/steps/\allowbreak 15\_coalescent\_model}). Fits were scored by $L_1$, the summed absolute difference between observed and expected proportions across the 20 frequency classes, because with $8.5\times10^{7}$ projected sites multinomial likelihood differences are dominated by sample size. $L_1$ is 0.036 for Kingman with growth (singleton fraction 0.481 against 0.483 observed), 0.108 for the best Beta coalescent ($\alpha=1.35$, singleton fraction 0.516) and 0.537 for constant Kingman.

\paragraph{Genotype-based statistics.}
Statistics that need individual genotypes were computed with pg\_gpu \citep{pggpu} from the filtered VCF.
Windowed $\pi$, $\dxy$ and Hudson $\Fst$ between arrangements used biallelic SNPs in 100-kb windows, with the 2a/2a
($n=223$) and 2+/2+ ($n=97$) homozygotes as the two populations and missing genotypes included pairwise. The between-division $\Fst$ matrix (Hudson), the linkage-disequilibrium triangles (genotype $r^2$ among common SNPs), and the principal-component analyses were computed the same way. The per-SNP geographic $\Fst$ scans (Supplementary Figs.~S3, S4) used plink2 \citep{chang2015plink2} to filter and count alleles at every biallelic SNP with a minor-allele frequency above 0.05 in the pooled 620 individuals from the ten divisions with more than one sample, the 618 retained and the two excluded afterwards (division 3N, with a single individual, was excluded). Per-division allele counts were converted to Hudson $\Fst$ for each of the 45 division pairs \citep{bhatia2013}, at sites genotyped in at least 40 individuals in that pair and with at least two alleles in each division. The per-SNP value is the ratio of averages over informative pairs (the sum of the numerators over the sum of the denominators), without clipping, keeping sites informative in at least 20 of the 45 pairs. Of the 23{,}892{,}020 SNPs passing the frequency filter, 20{,}276{,}930 met this criterion, 20{,}117{,}984 of them on the 45 autosomes (133{,}957 in the chromosome-2 inversion) and 158{,}946 on the Z chromosome. The null was built by shuffling division labels among the individuals 20 times (division sizes kept) and recomputing $\Fst$ at the same SNPs in the same way. Thresholds are quantiles of the pooled null over the autosomes outside the chromosome-2 inversion ($3.996\times10^{8}$ values), and the expected count above each threshold is the number of SNPs times the null tail rate.

Between-division $\Fst$ values (main-text Fig.~6c) are slightly negative and scale with the inverse of the division sample sizes (Pearson $r=-0.98$ with $1/n_1+1/n_2$), so they reflect sample size rather than structure. Because Tajima's $D$ depends on sample size under population growth, the division medians in Fig.~6 ($-1.26$ to $-1.64$, at most 25 individuals per division) are not directly comparable to the genome-wide $D$ of $-2.07$ from all individuals, and projecting the genome-wide spectrum to 25 individuals gives $D=-1.50$.

\paragraph{Genotype quality and karyotype groups.}
At about 2.5-fold depth, heterozygotes are under-called. On chromosome~20, over the biallelic PASS SNPs of the 619 individuals, the number of called heterozygotes is 0.36 of that expected under Hardy--Weinberg from the called allele frequencies (0.34 at minor-allele frequency $\geq$0.05, where 66\% of genotypes are called), with $H_e=2pq\,2n/(2n-1)$ per site from the $n$ called genotypes and $H_o/H_e=\sum H_o/\sum H_e$ over sites. Per individual, $H_o/H_e$ rises with read count from 0.14 in the lowest to 0.48 in the highest fifth (Spearman $\rho=0.99$), and so does the call rate ($\rho=0.99$, \cref{sfig:karyoqc}). The same estimator gives $H_o/H_e=0.32$ for each arrangement in the inversion and sets the heterozygote miscall rate $h=1-H_o/H_e$ of the genotype-error model in the simulations. The three karyotype groups do not differ in read count (medians 56.8, 59.8 and 53.7 million for 2a/2a, 2a/2+ and 2+/2+, Kruskal--Wallis $p=0.13$), chromosome-20 call rate ($p=0.09$) or $H_o/H_e$ ($p=0.13$), and karyotype is independent of division ($\chi^2$ $p=0.72$, the ten divisions with at least five individuals). With 97 2a/2a individuals matched to the 97 2+/2+ on read count (medians 53.5 and 53.7 million), $\pi_I/\pi_S$ is $0.708\pm0.027$ and $\dxy/\pi_S$ is $1.403\pm0.023$ over the same 189 windows, against $0.719\pm0.027$ and $1.414\pm0.024$ for all individuals, and from the ANGSD genotype likelihoods of the two groups $\pi_I/\pi_S$ is $0.704\pm0.026$. In the collinear flanks of chromosome~2 the groups have the same $\pi$ (ratio 1.00, and 0.98 when matched on depth). The 40 individuals of each homokaryotype used for the joint spectra differ in sampling event (33 of the 2a/2a from division 3K against 20 of the 2+/2+), which may account for the excess of private rare variants in 2+/2+ in the collinear control (\texttt{pipeline/steps/03\_karyotype/m4\_qc/}).

\paragraph{The inversion.}
Karyotypes were called with a three-component Gaussian mixture on the first principal component of a PCA of
chr2:60--80~Mb, fitted on the 619 individuals with the biallelic SNPs of minor-allele frequency above 0.05 among them (84{,}218 SNPs). To control for sequencing depth, every alignment was downsampled to about 1$\times$ in chr2:60--80~Mb (samtools, fixed seed), re-genotyped (77{,}209 SNPs), and karyotyped again in the same way. The two assignments agree for 617 of the 619 individuals (99.7\%). The two disagreements, full-depth 2a/2a and 2+/2+ calls that are 2a/2+ at equal depth, have read depth near the median and inversion heterozygosity typical of 2a/2+ (0.062 and 0.070, against medians of 0.078 in 2a/2+ and 0.033 and 0.040 in the homozygotes). The final calls take the equal-depth assignment for both (\texttt{data/qc/karyotypes\_619.tsv}), giving 223 2a/2a, 299 2a/2+ and 97 2+/2+.
Breakpoints were placed at the step change in between-arrangement differentiation and confirmed by a strand flip
in the AnchorWave alignment to \coin{}. The arrangement carried by each haplotype of the reference individual was
read from minimap2 2.29 (\texttt{-x asm5}) alignments of chromosome~2 of the two haplotype assemblies to each other
and to the primary assembly, and the orientation of each haplotype relative to \coin{} from alignments to the
homologous \coin{} chromosome (OZ346549.1, \texttt{-x asm20}). Alignment blocks of at least 1~Mb were ordered along
each \illex{} haplotype, and polarity was read from which arrangement keeps the order and adjacency of \coin{}
sequence across the rearranged segment (Supplementary Table~S3). The same comparison was made with the chromosome-level assembly of \textit{Sthenoteuthis oualaniensis} \citep{li2022flyingsquid}, a member of the same family. Its chromosome-2 homolog, HiC\_scaffold\_1, is the scaffold that receives 265 of the 319 \illex{} chromosome-2 genes transferred with LiftOn \citep{chao2024lifton}, and chromosome~2 of each haplotype assembly was aligned to it with minimap2 (\texttt{-x asm20}). Because the two genera differ by many small rearrangements along the chromosome, polarity was read at each breakpoint from which flanking segments are joined, using alignments of at least 5~kb with mapping quality of at least 5 together with the order of the transferred genes. Allele sharing with \coin{} was also scored at the 15{,}093 sites in the inversion where allele frequency differs by at least 0.7 between 2a/2a and 2+/2+ homozygotes, with the \coin{} base read from the AnchorWave alignment of \coin{} to the primary assembly, which carries 2+ in the inversion. Where the \coin{} base was callable (14{,}650 sites), the majority allele of 2a matches it at 26.0\% of sites, so 2+ carries the \coin{} allele at most diagnostic sites. The 2a matches are consistent with variation that was segregating in the ancestral population and was captured by the inversion when it formed, possibly in more than one capture event, which would give the soft start discussed in the main text. Chromosome~2 of both outgroups is rearranged in this region, so the arrangement of the common ancestor away from the breakpoints cannot be determined. It may have been an intermediate rearranged form, and the polarity used here is the reading on which \coin{} and \textit{S.~oualaniensis} agree at both breakpoints. Restricted to the 4{,}582 sites where \coin{} differs from the reference base, 2a carries the \coin{} allele at 65.0\% (2{,}980) and 2+ at 35.0\% (1{,}602), but this subset is selected for sites at which the reference, a 2+ haplotype, carries a non-\coin{} allele, so it is biased toward 2a matches. Reads of ten \textit{I.\ argentinus} individuals (about 0.3$\times$ each) mapped to the primary assembly were counted at the same diagnostic sites (\texttt{bcftools mpileup}, mapping and base quality of at least 20). Reference bias was measured at 150{,}000 SNPs in the collinear control (10--30~Mb), where the \textit{I.\ argentinus} non-reference read fraction tracks the \illex{} non-reference allele frequency $p$ as $0.018+1.02p$, and the frequency of 2a in \textit{I.\ argentinus} was estimated by maximum likelihood from the read counts under this calibration. All ten individuals give the same estimate (0.27--0.30, pooled 0.284), rather than the values near 0, 0.5 and 1 expected if the inversion segregated in \textit{I.\ argentinus}. At sites covered by at least four reads, 87\% carry reads of only one allele, as expected if each site is fixed, so \textit{I.\ argentinus} carries the 2+ allele at most diagnostic sites and the 2a allele at the rest. Its 2a alleles fall mainly at sites where \coin{} also carries the 2a allele (0.68 of reads, against 0.24 where \coin{} carries the 2+ allele), so they are best read as ancestral states shared by both species, and the inversion polymorphism is not shared with \textit{I.\ argentinus}. The age and frequency trajectory were fitted with msinv as described in the next paragraphs. Unannotated, non-repeat sequence in the inversion was searched with DIAMOND \texttt{blastx} against seven cephalopod proteomes, with transposable elements from Earl~Grey \citep{baril2024earlgrey} and transcripts from StringTie2 \citep{kovaka2019stringtie} as supporting evidence. This recovered 14 likely protein-coding loci that the annotation lacks. They are provided as \texttt{protein\_match} features in version 3 of the annotation and are not counted among the 89 gene models of the inversion. Fixed differences were sites with an allele-frequency difference of at least 0.9
between 2a/2a and 2+/2+ homozygotes. Gene-ontology enrichment of the inversion genes used Fisher's exact test against
the annotated-gene background in all three ontologies, compared with 500 random gene sets of equal size, and gene
density was compared with all autosomal windows of the same length (0.5-Mb steps). The zero-fold fraction among the top 1\% of coding sites by
per-site $\Fst$ (genotype likelihoods, 40 individuals per karyotype) was compared with all coding sites of the same
region by Fisher's exact test, in the inversion ($p=1\times10^{-32}$) and in collinear chr2:10--30~Mb ($p=0.007$).
Per-transcript $\dxy$, the mean between-arrangement divergence over a transcript's coding sites (transcripts with at
least 50), was normalised to its 100-kb window and compared with the same ratio for collinear control transcripts.

\paragraph{Inversion coalescent model.}
The inversion was simulated with msinv v0.4.1 (\url{https://github.com/stsmall/msinv}), a coalescent simulator with recombination that writes tree sequences. It uses the sequentially Markov approximation \citep{mcvean2005smc}, extended to two karyotype classes as in \citet{peischl2013}, in which two lineages coalesce only where they share ancestral material. The model code, inference scripts and every result quoted here are in the \texttt{illex/} and \texttt{results/illex/} directories of that repository (branch \texttt{illex/baseline-refit-karyofix}). Inside the inversion every lineage carries a karyotype label, ancestral (S, the 2+ arrangement) or derived (I, the 2a arrangement). Two lineages of the same class coalesce at rate $1/[2N(t)\,p_c(t)]$ per generation, where $N(t)$ is the effective size and $p_c(t)$ the frequency of their class, and a lineage of class $c$ recombines within the inversion at rate $r\,p_c(t)$, because crossing over occurs only between chromosomes of the same arrangement. Lineages of different classes cannot coalesce while the barrier exists, except through gene flux, which copies tracts of mean length 300~bp between arrangements, within the 188--390~bp estimated by \citet{korunes2019}, with the position-dependent initiation rate of \citet{peischl2013}, rising from zero at the breakpoints toward the centre of the inversion. The gene-flux parameter is the per-site conversion probability per generation in heterokaryotypes. Backward in time, the barrier is removed at $t_\mathrm{inv}$, when the derived lineages rejoin the ancestral class. Each proposal simulated 40~kb of inversion between two 10~kb collinear margins, and a separate 40~kb of collinear sequence with msprime 1.4.1 \citep{baumdicker2022} as the control, with 100 chromosomes of each arrangement and $\mu=3\times10^{-9}$. For linkage disequilibrium the chromosomes of each arrangement were paired at random into 50 diploids and passed through the genotype error model of the data, in which a heterozygote is called homozygous with probability $h$ and each genotype is missing with probability one minus the call rate, both estimated for each arrangement and region from the same individuals as the observed statistics (call rates 0.62--0.67, $h=0.64$--0.69).

\paragraph{Neutral baseline.}
The demography and recombination rate of the simulations were fitted to collinear chromosome-2 sequence flanking the inversion, chr2:40--55 and 85--115~Mb, by rejection ABC. This sequence lies 5.5--35.5~Mb outside the breakpoints and avoids a stretch of doubled diversity at chr2:24.1--35.9~Mb. The 2a/2a and 2+/2+ individuals were treated as two random sample sets, and the fitted statistics were the folded site-frequency spectrum in six bins, linkage disequilibrium in six distance bins, Tajima's $D$ and $\log_{10}\pi$, each averaged over the two sample sets, together with eight cells of the joint spectrum between them (the four cells for variants near fixation in one set and absent from the other are empty in collinear sequence and were left out). Diversity in this sequence, from the summed within-group ANGSD spectra, is $\pi=0.0061$, against 0.0093 for the autosomes. The model was exponential growth from $N_\mathrm{anc}$ to $N_0$ over $T_\mathrm{grow}$ generations, constant before that, with recombination rate $r$ and $\mu$ fixed at $3\times10^{-9}$. 20{,}000 draws from log-uniform priors ($N_\mathrm{anc}$ $10^{5}$--$3\times10^{6}$, growth fold 1--200, $T_\mathrm{grow}$ $10^{4}$--$3\times10^{6}$, $r$ $10^{-10}$--$10^{-8}$) were each simulated for 200~kb with msprime and the control's genotype error model. The 1\% closest to the data in a MAD-scaled Euclidean distance were accepted with Epanechnikov weights, and the log parameters were adjusted by local-linear regression \citep{beaumont2002}. The posterior medians (95\% intervals) are $N_\mathrm{anc}=507{,}288$ (378{,}143--635{,}672), $N_0=9{,}112{,}696$ (7{,}632{,}032--11{,}683{,}078), $T_\mathrm{grow}=382{,}957$ (314{,}983--472{,}238) and $r=5.51\times10^{-10}$ ($4.61$--$6.49\times10^{-10}$). With $\mu$ fixed, only $r/\mu$ is identified, so $r$ is the effective rate for these statistics. The accepted draws reproduce the spectrum, Tajima's $D$, $\pi$, linkage disequilibrium from 240~bp to 20~kb, and the symmetric cells of the joint spectrum. Linkage disequilibrium at 100--240~bp lies above every accepted draw, and 2+/2+ carries more private rare variants than 2a/2a (0.352 against 0.272 of variable sites), which a single panmictic population does not produce. The inversion model used the posterior medians.

\paragraph{Trajectory models and priors.}
The frequency of 2a followed a Wright--Fisher diffusion in steps of 10 generations, with the effective size of the neutral baseline. Under neutral drift (N) there is no selection. Under directional selection the frequency changes deterministically by $s\,p(1-p)$ per generation from $t_\mathrm{sel}$ to the present (D1) or only until $t_\mathrm{stop}$, with drift alone after that (D2). Under balancing selection (B) it changes by $s\,p(1-p)(p^{*}-p)/p^{*}$ from $t_\mathrm{sel}$, the form expected under overdominance with equilibrium $p^{*}$. A hard start places a single copy of 2a at $t_\mathrm{inv}$, with selection from $t_\mathrm{inv}$, and its path is weighted by the exact probability that the copy establishes. A soft start places 2a at a founding frequency $p_0$ at $t_\mathrm{inv}$, with neutral drift until $t_\mathrm{sel}$. A path is kept only if 2a is neither lost nor fixed and ends within 0.02 of its present frequency of 0.602. The priors were $t_\mathrm{inv}$ uniform on $10^{5}$--$1.5\times10^{6}$ generations, $p_0$ log-uniform on $10^{-3}$--0.10, $t_\mathrm{sel}=f\,t_\mathrm{inv}$ and $t_\mathrm{stop}=f'\,t_\mathrm{sel}$ with $f$ and $f'$ uniform on 0--1, $s$ log-uniform on $10^{-6}$--$10^{-1}$, $p^{*}$ uniform on 0.3--0.95, the gene-flux rate log-uniform on $10^{-9}$--$10^{-5}$ when gene flux was fitted and $10^{-15}$ otherwise, and equal prior probabilities of a hard and a soft start. The upper bound of 0.10 on $p_0$, a sixth of the present frequency of 2a, keeps a soft start an arrangement that is still rare when the barrier forms, so that its rise to 0.602 is inferred rather than built into the start. Because the posterior of $p_0$ reaches this bound (\cref{sfig:npepost}), the founding frequency is constrained by the prior above about 0.05.

\paragraph{Summary statistics.}
Thirty-nine statistics were fitted. Three are ratios between arrangements, $\pi_I/\pi_S$, $\dxy/\pi_I$ and the ratio of their singleton-class fractions. Twelve are the folded site-frequency spectra of the two arrangements projected to 20 chromosomes, as proportions of segregating sites in bins 1 to 5 and 6 or more. Twelve are the mean genotype $r^{2}$ between SNPs with a minor-allele frequency of at least 0.05 within each arrangement, in six log-spaced distance bins from 100~bp to 20~kb. Because unphased genotype $r^{2}$ depends on genotype error, the simulated genotypes carry the same heterozygote-dropout factor $h$ and call rate as the data, estimated per arrangement from the observed ratio of observed to expected heterozygosity ($h=0.68$ in both arrangements). Twelve are the cells of the folded joint spectrum, with both arrangements projected to 20 chromosomes and binned at 0, 1--4, 5--15, 16--19 and 20 derived copies. Every statistic except the joint spectrum is divided by the same statistic in the control, in the simulations and in the data alike. In the data the ratios come from called genotypes of the 223 2a/2a and 97 2+/2+ individuals (pg\_gpu), the spectra from per-arrangement ANGSD site-frequency spectra, the joint spectrum from a two-dimensional realSFS spectrum of 40 individuals of each homokaryotype (the first 40 of each karyotype list, with four 2a/2a individuals re-karyotyped by the depth-equalised call replaced by random 2a/2a individuals, seed 20261111; the cap keeps the two-dimensional estimation tractable), and linkage disequilibrium from 20 random subsamples of 50 individuals of each arrangement. The control is the collinear sequence of the neutral baseline, and its joint spectrum was built with the same 80 individuals, sites and filters as the inversion's. Three further statistics were withheld from the fit and used only to check it, the Tajima's $D$ of each arrangement and the ratio of $\dxy/\pi$ in windows near the breakpoints to that in windows at the centre of the inversion.

\paragraph{ABC random-forest model choice.}
The four histories were compared by ABC-RF \citep{pudlo2016} in abcrf, on a reference table simulated from the priors of the four histories with gene flux, with hard and soft starts simulated separately and each path kept when it passed the present-frequency filter (16{,}959 simulations after 19 with undefined statistics were removed, 3{,}228 for N, 3{,}744 for D1, 4{,}994 for D2 and 4{,}993 for B). Hard starts were weighted by their establishment probability, and each start class by its prior probability and its acceptance rate, so that each history carries its prior-weighted mass. A forest of 1{,}000 trees was grown on the 39 statistics and their linear discriminant axes, and the posterior probability of each history is the prior-weighted mass of the training simulations that share leaves with the observed statistics, multiplied by the probability that a prior draw of the history passes the present-frequency filter (0.0064 for B and 0.0005--0.0006 for each of the others). At the observed statistics the forest alone gives 0.31 for D2, 0.29 for B, 0.24 for N and 0.17 for D1, and with the acceptance rates the posterior probabilities are 0.83 for B, 0.08 for D2, 0.06 for N and 0.04 for D1. The classifier was assessed by grouped five-fold cross-validation (Supplementary Fig.~S9). Its accuracy is 0.51 against 0.25 by chance. Neutral drift and ongoing directional selection are recovered best (0.86 and 0.68), whereas balancing selection is recovered in 0.36 of its mass and confused mainly with neutral drift (0.33), and a stalled sweep is recovered in 0.14 and confused with the other three.

\paragraph{Simulated length.}
Each simulation covers 40~kb of inversion, against 18.96~Mb in the data. To test whether this length represents the whole inversion, 48 NPE posterior draws of the balancing model with gene flux were each given one frequency path, and the same path was simulated over 40~kb, 160~kb, 640~kb, 2.56~Mb and the full 18.96~Mb. For the 47 draws with a valid path (one draw produced none within 200 attempts), the mean of every fitted and withheld statistic at 18.96~Mb lies within 0.2 standard deviations (of the 40-kb values across draws) of its mean at 40~kb. The spread across draws is smaller at 18.96~Mb, 0.3--1.0 of that at 40~kb for the fitted statistics and 0.12 for the edge-to-centre ratio. The 40-kb simulations therefore reproduce the expected statistics of the full inversion with more variance, which widens the posterior rather than shifting it.

\paragraph{Neural posterior estimation.}
Parameters of the balancing model were estimated by neural posterior estimation \citep{cranmer2020,min2026} in sbi 0.27 \citep{tejero2020,boelts2025}. The training simulations were new draws from the prior of the balancing model with a founding frequency, with and without gene flux, kept when the present frequency of 2a fell within 0.02 of 0.602, with the same simulator, baseline and 39 statistics (59{,}743 simulations with gene flux and 59{,}531 without, each from about 5.4 million prior draws). Starts from a single copy, which carry 3\% of the posterior mass of balancing selection in the ABC-RF reference table, were not included. The parameters were transformed to the scale on which their priors are uniform ($t_\mathrm{inv}$, $t_\mathrm{sel}/t_\mathrm{inv}$, $\log_{10}p_0$, $\log_{10}s$, $p^{*}$ and the log gene-flux rate), and the statistics were standardised. The posterior is an equal mixture of five neural spline flows \citep{durkan2019} with five transforms and 64 hidden units, each trained from a different seed with 10\% of the training set held for validation and stopped after 30 epochs without improvement. Calibration was tested on 2{,}000 held-out simulations by simulation-based calibration \citep{talts2018} and by the TARP coverage test \citep{lemos2023}. With gene flux the central 50, 90 and 95\% intervals contain the true value in 49--52, 89--91 and 94--96\% of simulations, and the rank distributions are uniform (Kolmogorov--Smirnov $p$ 0.12--0.68) except for a slight deviation for $p^{*}$ ($p=0.02$). The observed statistics lie inside the training distribution, at the 0.27 quantile of the robust Mahalanobis distance and between the 29th and 89th percentile for each statistic. Without gene flux the coverage is also nominal and the ranks are uniform ($p$ 0.09--0.64), but about half of the training simulations have no variants shared at low frequency by the two arrangements, which the data have. Twenty thousand posterior draws were taken at the observed statistics. The trajectories in main-text Fig.~4b and Supplementary Fig.~S10 were simulated from 400 posterior draws with gene flux, each re-simulated until its path passed the present-frequency filter (392 within 50 attempts).

\paragraph{Posterior predictive checks.}
The balancing model was checked from 1{,}000 NPE posterior draws for each gene-flux setting. Each draw was simulated again with the same simulator, with the frequency path retried until it passed the present-frequency filter. Each statistic was scored by the quantile of the observed value among the replicates and a two-sided predictive $p$-value, and the joint fit by the rank of the observed distance from the predictive median, in the distance of the fit, among the replicates' distances (Supplementary Fig.~S11 for the balancing model with gene flux). Under balancing selection with gene flux the joint predictive $p$ is 0.51 (0.09 without gene flux). The withheld statistics are reproduced (two-sided $p$ 0.84 and 0.56 for Tajima's $D$ in 2a and 2+, 0.99 for the edge-to-centre ratio). With gene flux, no statistic falls below $p=0.05$, and the cell of variants at low frequency in both arrangements, linkage disequilibrium at 8--20~kb in 2+ and at 100--580~bp in 2a, and the cell of variants rare in 2a and fixed in 2+ are at the edge of their ranges ($p=0.07$--0.14). Without gene flux, no statistic falls below $p=0.05$ either, and linkage disequilibrium at 8--20~kb in 2+ ($p=0.07$) and the cells of variants rare in 2a and at high frequency or fixed in 2+ are at the edge of their ranges (observed 0.016 and 0.011, predicted 0.002 and 0.0002, $p=0.12$ and 0.06). The cell of variants at low frequency in both arrangements is reproduced in both settings (observed 0.064, predicted 0.031 with gene flux and 0.035 without, $p=0.07$ and 0.29). In the collinear flanks the same cell matches the neutral baseline (observed 0.233, accepted draws 0.210--0.269), so the two-dimensional spectrum and the baseline reproduce it where there is no barrier.

\paragraph{Recombination map.}
ReLERNN \citep{adrion2020relernn} was run in unphased mode on biallelic SNPs with at most 50\% missing genotypes.
Individuals with no genotype calls on a chromosome cannot enter the unphased model, and the 329 confidently sexed males
with data were used. The autosomal network was trained on chromosomes 1, 13 and 45 with
12{,}000 coalescent simulations (with 1{,}000 each for validation and testing) under the Stairway~Plot~2
demography with $\mu=3\times10^{-9}$, an upper bound of 4 on the ratio of $\rho$ to $\theta$, and 6-kb windows,
masked by the inaccessible regions and the 22 $\pi$-elevated blocks with a window-level masking threshold of 0.9.
It was trained for 50 epochs and applied to every autosome except chromosome~2, in windows of 3--6~kb. The Z
network was trained in the same way in 30-kb windows, with the Z accessibility mask and the effective sizes of the
demography multiplied by 0.60, the Z-to-autosome diversity ratio. Predictions were corrected with 200
parametric-bootstrap replicates in each of 20 rate slices. The map pools all karyotypes and reads linkage
disequilibrium within windows of a few kilobases, so it does not resolve the barrier between chromosome-2 arrangements, which is shown instead by long-range linkage
disequilibrium (Supplementary Figs.~S8, S19).

\paragraph{Z recombination and sex-biased mutation.}
Linkage disequilibrium on the Z and on the autosomes was compared in the 329 ZZ males, the only individuals that
carry two Z copies. Two-locus statistics ($D^2$ and $\pi_2$, unbiased for unphased genotypes
\citep{ragsdale2020unphased}) were computed with pg\_gpu \citep{pggpu} on accessible biallelic SNPs in 2-Mb windows,
86 on the autosomes (at 10 and 40~Mb of each autosome, excluding the chromosome-2 inversion and the
anomalous-diversity blocks) and nine spaced every 4~Mb along the Z, with SNP pairs binned by distance from 0 to
5~kb. The decay of $\sigma_D^2=D^2/\pi_2$ with distance was fitted to the expectation of \citet{ohta1971} plus a
constant sample-size term, giving the population-scaled rate $\rho=4N_e r$ per base for each chromosome class. The
ratio $\rho_Z/\rho_A$ was also estimated without a model, as the distance rescaling that best superimposes the two
decay curves. Confidence intervals come from 200 bootstrap resamples of windows. Because the Z recombines only in
males and spends two thirds of its time in them, $\rho_Z/\rho_A=(N_{e,Z}/N_{e,A})\,(4/3)\,r_m/(r_m+r_f)$, where
$r_m$ and $r_f$ are the male and female per-generation rates and the male rate is taken to be the same on the Z
and the autosomes. With $N_{e,Z}/N_{e,A}=3/4$, equal recombination in the two sexes predicts $\rho_Z/\rho_A=0.5$
and achiasmate female meiosis predicts 1 \citep{coffing2026}.

Sex-biased mutation was estimated from synonymous divergence between \illex{} and \textit{S.~oualaniensis}, whose chromosome~46 is syntenic with the \illex{} Z \citep{small2026}. The \illex{} annotation was transferred to the
\textit{S.~oualaniensis} assembly with LiftOn \citep{chao2024lifton}, one transcript per gene was aligned codon by
codon with MACSE 2.07 \citep{ranwez2018macse}, which allows frameshifts in the transferred sequence, and codons
with gaps, frameshifts, ambiguous bases or stop codons were removed. Synonymous and nonsynonymous divergence
($d_S$, $d_N$) were computed per gene by the method of \citet{nei1986} with a Jukes--Cantor correction. Genes in
the chromosome-2 inversion or the anomalous-diversity blocks were excluded, leaving 8{,}649 autosomal and 109
Z-linked genes with $d_S>0$. The male-to-female mutation ratio $\alpha=\mu_m/\mu_f$ was obtained from the ratio
$R$ of mean Z to mean autosomal $d_S$ as $\alpha=(3R-2)/(4-3R)$, following \citet{miyata1987}, as in \citet{coffing2025} and
\citet{coffing2026}. Z-linked genes carry more gene-body CpG methylation than autosomal genes in \illex{}, most strongly in females \citep{small2026}. If this extends to the germline, methylation-associated mutation raises the Z rate and biases $\alpha$ upward, so the female bias is if anything underestimated. To separate mutation from selective constraint and base composition, $R$ was recomputed with
autosomal genes matched to the Z genes by deciles of $d_N/d_S$ and of third-position GC content (GC3), and with
genes restricted to those above the autosomal median $d_N/d_S$. Confidence intervals come from 2{,}000 bootstrap
resamples of genes within strata.

Linkage disequilibrium decays at the same rate on the Z as on the autosomes, $\rho_Z/\rho_A=1.04$ (95\% CI
0.88--1.28) from the decay fit and 1.08 (0.78--1.67) from curve rescaling, with $\rho_A=0.026$ per base
(Supplementary Table~S9). Synonymous divergence is lower on the Z ($d_S=0.298$ vs 0.358, Mann--Whitney $p=0.009$),
giving $R=0.83$ and $\alpha=0.33$ (95\% CI 0.10--0.70), a female mutation rate about three times the male rate.
Z-linked genes are slightly more constrained (median $d_N/d_S$ 0.082 vs 0.113, $p=0.048$) and lower in GC3 (0.36
vs 0.42, $p<10^{-16}$). Matching on $d_N/d_S$ leaves $\alpha$ unchanged (0.36), and matching on GC3 raises it to
0.43, or 0.49 when combined with $d_N/d_S$, so the female bias holds under each control, with the upper
confidence limit reaching 1 only under joint matching. Restricting to genes above the autosomal median $d_N/d_S$ leaves only 44 Z genes and gives $\alpha=0.24$ (0--0.65), in line with the other estimates but less precise. Because $R$ estimates $\mu_Z/\mu_A$, the ratio of male Z
to autosomal diversity ($\pi_Z/\pi_A=0.00563/0.0093=0.61$) implies $N_{e,Z}/N_{e,A}=0.68$--0.76 across these
estimates, close to the 3/4 expected under an equal breeding sex ratio. Combined with the
linkage-disequilibrium ratio, this gives $r_m/(r_m+r_f)\approx1.02$--1.14, consistent with achiasmate female
meiosis \citep{coffing2026} and inconsistent with equal recombination in the two sexes, which predicts 0.5.

\paragraph{Selective-sweep scan.}
diploS/HIC used 1.1-Mb feature windows of eleven 100-kb sub-windows and twelve diploid statistics per
sub-window. It was trained on hard and soft sweeps simulated in SLiM~5.2 and recapitated with msprime, and on
neutral msprime simulations, all under the fitted growth demography, at the empirical sample size, and with the
empirical per-individual missing-data structure. The final model classified held-out simulations with 81\%
accuracy (Supplementary Fig.~S20). Applied to the genome it assigns 1\% of windows to the neutral class and 91\% to
the linked classes, because real neutral windows carry three to eight times more spatial heterogeneity in
diversity among sub-windows than the simulations. We therefore used it as an outlier detector. The sweep score
$p_\mathrm{hard}+p_\mathrm{soft}$ was ranked within strata of callable fraction (quintiles) and coding density
(zero or not), and windows in the top 1\% of their stratum were outliers. RAiSD and SweepFinder2 statistics were
ranked the same way, and a region was concordant when a diploS/HIC outlier was supported by either. The
false-discovery rate of the concordant set was estimated by shifting the joint RAiSD and SweepFinder2 outlier track
circularly within each chromosome by an independent uniform offset of whole 100-kb windows, 10{,}000 times, and
counting the diploS/HIC outlier regions that became concordant by chance (227 outlier regions, 446 footprint
outlier windows among 22{,}278 callable windows). The mean of this null, 4.8 regions (95\% range 1--9), against the
24 observed gives a false-discovery rate of 0.20 (no shift reached 24). Tier-1 calls are stratified outliers by
construction and have no comparable estimate. Tier-1 calls
came from a hidden Markov model with neutral, linked and sweep-centre states, whose emissions are the
prior-corrected class posteriors and whose dwell lengths follow the $\sim$400-kb sweep footprint scaled by the
recombination map. The decoder is not calibrated by simulation, because at this population size the simulated ancestral recombination graphs carry about $3.4\times10^{5}$ marginal trees per 2.5~Mb. Its settings are design-based and were checked against the concordant regions (Supplementary Fig.~S22). The background-selection screen compared each call's $\pi$, from the ANGSD windows it overlaps, with the value predicted from the fraction of coding sites within 1~Mb of its midpoint, $\hat\pi=0.010593-0.165473\,f_\mathrm{CDS}$, the genome-wide linear relationship between the two. A call was retained when $\pi-\hat\pi\leq-0.003$, i.e.\ when its diversity is at least 0.003 below the background-selection expectation. Retained calls with $f_\mathrm{CDS}$ above the genome median are those well below the expectation, and those below it are low-diversity calls in gene-poor sequence where background selection is weak.
The target of each of the 11 retained autosomal candidates was localised with the ANGSD 10-kb windows (\texttt{pipeline/steps/14\_sweep\_seqmodel/scripts/harness/auto\_finescan.py}). The window of lowest $\pi$ within 0.5~Mb of the call's midpoint was taken as the centre of the sweep, and the annotated gene within 7.5~kb of it as the target. All 11 centres lie in the lowest 6\% of their chromosome's $\pi$, with Tajima's $D$ of $-2.70$ to $-2.42$. Three fall inside the 100-kb call and eight 65--475~kb beside it, and two of these move the target to a neighbouring gene (\textit{Cpes} at chr1:85.5~Mb, about 11~kb from a centre in a gene desert, and \textit{GALT} at chr25:65.0~Mb). The Z candidates were localised in the same way from 10-kb windows of $\pi$ and Tajima's $D$ computed from the called genotypes of the males (\texttt{chrZ\_finescan.py}).
Gene-ontology enrichment used genes with EnTAP \citep{hart2020entap} annotations that overlap a call, against all
annotated genes outside the Z and the masked blocks.

\paragraph{Anomalous-diversity blocks.}
Blocks were called where the rolling median of 10-kb $\pi$ exceeded twice the chromosome median over at least
1~Mb. Each was characterised by per-division $\pi$, between-division $\Fst$, local PCA, and genotype linkage
disequilibrium, and by read depth relative to 5-Mb flanks in all 621 individuals (samtools bedcov at mapping
quality 0 and 20). Long-read depth and heterozygous-site allele fractions came from PacBio HiFi reads of the
assembly individual and a second individual aligned to the assembly, and genotype-likelihood $\pi$ was
recomputed at mapping quality 60. Blocks with population depth at least 1.15 times their flanks were classed as
collapsed duplications and those at 0.90 or less as under-mapped, and the fraction of each block's accessible sequence that is not uniquely mappable was taken from GenMap (Supplementary Table~S5).

    $\rho=+0.02$, unchanged when $\pi$ is held fixed, partial $\rho=-0.01$), so recombination is agnostic to gene content, as it should be. \textbf{(b)}~Nucleotide diversity $\pi$, by contrast, rises steeply with
    distance from coding sequence ($\rho=+0.29$, +43\% near$\to$far), the footprint of linked selection.
    \textbf{(c)}~$\pi$ falls with local coding density ($\rho=-0.27$), as background selection reduces diversity where genes are dense, i.e.\ a locally reduced effective size, and it is
    gene-density-driven. Linked selection in \illex{} is thus structured by gene density and gene
    proximity rather than by the recombination-rate axis, which is nearly uniform genome-wide
    (per-chromosome medians 0.23--0.26~cM/Mb, Supplementary Figs.~S14, S16).}
  \label{sfig:recombsel}
