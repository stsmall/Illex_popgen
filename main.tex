\begin{abstract}
\noindent
The northern shortfin squid \illex{} is a short-lived, highly fecund, broadcast-spawning
cephalopod that supports major commercial fisheries across the North Atlantic, yet its
genome-scale population history and the targets of natural selection are unknown. We
analysed a 350-individual, whole-genome callset spanning 45 autosomes and the Z chromosome.
The population carries the signature of a strong, ongoing demographic expansion --- genome-wide
nucleotide diversity $\pi\approx0.0093$ with Tajima's $D\approx-2$ in essentially every window ---
and a moments-fit history of a $\sim$12-fold size increase from an ancestral $\Ne\approx5.5\times10^{5}$
to a present $\Ne\approx6.8\times10^{6}$. Consistent with a recent population-genomic survey
\citep{baker2025}, population structure is confined to a single large inversion on chromosome~2; here we go
beyond that description to establish what the inversion \emph{is} and to map the genome-wide landscape of
recent positive selection. The chr2 inversion (chr2:60.54--79.50~Mb,
$\sim$19~Mb) drives all apparent structure ($\Fst=0.37$ between arrangements, geographic $\Fst\approx0$),
is non-clinal, and is \emph{not} neutral: two mutation-rate-free arguments and a coalescent fit
with an ARG simulator that enforces the arrangement barrier show it arose $\sim$850~ka, was maintained
near a frequency of $\sim$0.74 for most of its history, and has been actively removed to its present
0.37 over the last $\sim$50--100~ky under selection of order $\Ne s\sim10^{2}$--$10^{3}$. We further
build a per-sex recombination map, quantify moderate gene-density-driven background selection,
identify candidate adaptive genes by McDonald--Kreitman tests, and perform a genome-wide selective-sweep
scan. Because a simulation-trained per-window classifier over-calls sweeps on real, expansion-shaped,
missing-data-rich genomes, we deploy it as a stratified empirical-outlier detector corroborated by a
second method, yielding a small, biologically coherent candidate set --- including neural and
RNA-regulatory genes, a cephalopod-lineage-specific gene under a hard sweep, and, on the Z chromosome,
a cluster of RNA-regulation and hormone-signalling loci. Our results establish \illex{} as a
large-$\Ne$, expansion-dominated system in which a single balancing-selected supergene is the sole
axis of structure, and provide a reusable, honestly-calibrated framework for sweep detection under
these conditions.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}

Cephalopods combine an unusual life history --- semelparity, enormous fecundity, near-total larval
mortality, and boom--bust recruitment --- with population sizes among the largest of any animal.
The northern shortfin squid, \illex{}, is an annual, semelparous broadcast spawner distributed across
the northwest Atlantic shelf and slope, where it supports large and economically important fisheries.
These traits make it a compelling but under-studied system for population genomics: extreme fecundity
with high early mortality is the demographic profile that motivates sweepstakes reproductive-success
models, in which a few individuals contribute disproportionately to each generation, while a large
effective size sharpens the resolution of both demographic inference and selection scans.

A recent population-genomic survey of \illex{} \citep{baker2025} established the population-structure
picture we confirm here --- weak overall differentiation with a single, striking axis of structure driven by
a chromosome-2 inversion, recovered by PCA, $\Fst$, and admixture analysis. That description leaves open the
questions that motivate this study: \emph{why} the inversion segregates as it does, and \emph{where else} in
the genome selection has acted. We therefore focus on two things that a structure-and-differentiation survey
does not resolve. First, the evolutionary character of the chr2 inversion: is it a neutral, drifting
polymorphism or a selected supergene, and if selected, of what kind, what age, and with what frequency
history? Second, the genome-wide landscape of recent positive selection --- the selective sweeps, on the
autosomes and the sex chromosome, that a differentiation scan cannot see. Underpinning both, we ask whether
\illex{}'s demography can be distinguished from the multiple-merger coalescent that its sweepstakes
reproduction would predict, since the answer licenses every age and selection estimate that follows.

We address these with a whole-genome dataset of 350 \illex{} individuals, supported by an \coin{} outgroup
for divergence-based inference. We infer demography from the site-frequency spectrum and linkage
disequilibrium, dissect the chr2 inversion with per-arrangement diversity, divergence, and a
purpose-built coalescent model of a barrier-bearing chromosome, build a per-sex recombination map,
quantify background selection, and scan for selective sweeps. Throughout, we treat the mismatch between
simulated and real neutral genomes not as a nuisance to be hidden but as a finding in its own right that
dictates how a sweep scan must be framed.

\section{Results}

\subsection{Sex determination and genome organisation}
We genotyped 633 \illex{} individuals sampled across eleven NAFO management divisions (\cref{fig:map}) spanning
$\sim$37--50$^\circ$N (\cref{tab:summary}). Coverage on the Z chromosome cleanly separates the sample into
\textbf{342 ZZ males and 291 ZO females}, consistent with a ZZ/ZO sex-determination system in which females
are the heterogametic sex, carrying a single Z with no W chromosome. A chromosome
previously suspected to be sex-linked (chr42) is autosomal by this criterion and is treated as such
throughout. All autosomal analyses use both sexes; the Z chromosome, on which males are diploid and females
haploid, is analysed separately (\cref{sec:sweep}).

\subsection{Population structure is confined to the chromosome-2 inversion (confirming prior work)}
Consistent with \citet{baker2025}, a windowed principal-component analysis finds population structure in
exactly one place in the genome: inside the chromosome-2 inversion, where the among-cluster variance explained
is $\eta^{2}=0.748$, against $\eta^{2}=0.003$ in collinear sequence and on every other chromosome
(\cref{fig:overview}). There is no cryptic geographic or cryptic-lineage structure elsewhere, and the residual
dispersion along the minor PCA axes is attributable to a handful of individuals rather than to any population
axis (it tracks neither division nor latitude nor coverage). A KING-robust relatedness screen of the autosomal
genotypes further finds \textbf{no cryptic family structure} --- no duplicate, parent--offspring, or full-sib
pairs and a single second-degree pair among the 633 individuals (maximum pairwise kinship $0.16$) --- so
despite the sweepstakes reproductive mode the sample behaves as an effectively unrelated set of individuals,
and relatedness cannot inflate the diversity or demographic inferences below. Because a per-SNP
differentiation scan can detect local adaptation that the diversity-based sweep tests would miss --- a
sensitive test in a population this panmictic --- we also compared per-SNP $\Fst$ among divisions to a
coalescent neutral null (a single panmictic population simulated under the fitted demography at the empirical
sample sizes and missingness): across the full callsets (551{,}344 SNPs covering 12{,}007 genes) the
empirical differentiation lies entirely within the drift expectation --- \emph{no} SNP exceeds the neutral
maximum, a single intergenic outlier passes the lenient threshold against $\approx$556 expected by chance
($\sim$555$\times$ fewer than the false-positive rate), and there are \emph{zero} genic outliers --- so there
is no signal of geographic local adaptation anywhere in the genome, and the chr2 inversion is geographically
non-clinal (Supplementary Figs.~S2, S19). We treat this
as an established starting point rather than a result: it confirms the earlier PCA/$\Fst$/admixture picture at
per-window resolution, means population structure cannot be invoked as a confounder for the analyses that
follow, and --- most importantly --- reframes the inversion from ``the thing that structures the population''
into an evolutionary object to be characterised in its own right (\cref{sec:inv}).

\subsection{A large, balancing-selected inversion on chromosome~2}\label{sec:inv}
The chr2 inversion is the central feature of the \illex{} genome. Local-PCA and a Gaussian-mixture
karyotype assignment (95.3\% confident) resolve three genotype clusters --- 254 AA, 284 AB, and 95 BB
individuals --- whose differentiation is karyotypic, not geographic: $\Fst(\mathrm{AA,BB})=0.37$ within
the inversion against a geographic $\Fst\approx0$. The three karyotypes co-occur throughout the sampled
range, with no latitudinal cline in arrangement frequency (0.29--0.46 across divisions; Supplementary
Fig.~S4 and Table~S2). Two independent lines pin the breakpoints to
\textbf{chr2:60.54--79.50~Mb} (a $\sim$18.96~Mb differentiated body): a step-change in arrangement
differentiation and a strand flip in a whole-chromosome alignment to \coin{}, which independently confirm
the right breakpoint. The nominal 60.0--80.0~Mb extent overshoots by $\sim$0.5~Mb at each end.

\paragraph{Polarisation.}
The derived, inverted arrangement is \textbf{BB, at a present frequency of 0.374}; AA is ancestral and
standard. This polarisation is the reverse of a naive one based on the reference allele, which is circular
because the \illex{} reference assembly is itself in the inverted orientation (it carries BB at 86.5\% of
fixed-different sites). Restricting to the non-circular subset of sites where \coin{} carries the \illex{}
alternate allele shows AA to be ancestral (63.0\%) and BB derived --- a single inversion event, with the
reference simply assembled from an inverted individual.

\paragraph{The inversion is not neutral.}
The primary evidence is per-arrangement diversity (\cref{tab:inv}, \cref{fig:inv}). The rare derived
arrangement is \emph{more} diverse than its current frequency can sustain: $\pi_I/\pi_S=1.356\pm0.048$
against an equilibrium expectation of $p/(1-p)=0.60$ for a polymorphism held at 0.374 --- a
2.3-fold \emph{excess}, the signature of an old polymorphism that was once commoner, not a recent rise.
Two mutation-rate-free diffusion arguments exclude neutrality. First, \emph{attainment}: reaching the
inferred historical peak frequency of $\sim$0.74 by drift takes at minimum $\sim$1.15~million generations
at the binding (smallest) $\Ne$, i.e.\ $\sim$1.36$\times$ the entire inferred lifetime of the inversion, and
up to $17\times$ at present $\Ne$. Second, the \emph{decline}: the observed fall of $0.74\to0.374$ is
6.7 standard deviations of neutral drift at the fitted duration, and a coalescent profile of the fall
duration excludes the slow, drift-compatible alternative at $\Delta\chi^{2}\gtrsim8$--80. The arrangement
was actively removed, not lost to drift.

\paragraph{Age and history.}
We fit the inversion's age and frequency trajectory with \textbf{msinv}, an ARG-based coalescent simulator
that enforces the cross-karyotype recombination barrier and outputs tree sequences (\cref{sec:methods}).
Conditioning on a deterministic frequency trajectory under within-arrangement neutrality --- the model the
data support, since they constrain the \emph{shape} of $p(t)$, not a site-level mechanism --- the joint fit
to $\pi_I/\pi_S$, $\dxy/\pi_I$, and the per-arrangement singleton spectrum (\cref{fig:demog}; Supplementary Fig.~S3) gives
a coalescent age of $\mathbf{\sim}$\textbf{850~ky}. The age is the robustly-determined quantity because it
is carried by $\dxy$ between arrangements, which is symmetric to the polarisation. The reconstructed history
is: the inversion arose $\sim$850~ka, coincident with the onset of the population expansion; was maintained
near $p\approx0.74$ for most of its history; and then fell sharply to 0.374 over $\sim$50--100~ky ending
$\gtrsim$50--100~ka, under selection of order $\Ne s\sim10^{2}$--$10^{3}$ ($s\approx1.5\times10^{-4}$).
Every absolute age scales inversely with the mutation rate $\mu=3\times10^{-9}$, which remains the dominant
external uncertainty.

\paragraph{What the inversion is not.}
Recombination inside the inversion is at the chromosomal background rate by direct measurement (the
$\sim$19~kb LD windows a recombination-map method reads are blind to the 19-Mb barrier, so the interior rate
is not a barrier readout), so the differentiation is a consequence of the barrier, not of locally suppressed
crossing-over. The region is gene-\emph{poor} (30 annotated genes in the differentiated body against a median
of 50 in random windows of the same size), its annotation is demonstrably incomplete ($\sim$12--14 genes
missed, recovered by cross-species protein homology), and a previously-reported metabolic Gene-Ontology
enrichment does not survive a linkage-aware null. No single locus stands out in coding sequence, regulatory
compartments, per-transcript divergence, or a karyotype-stratified sweep scan: the block is differentiated
as a unit, which is what a recombination barrier produces. Gene flux between arrangements is not detectable
in mean $\dxy$, though a residual $\sim$40\% depletion of near-fixed differences in the outermost megabase
remains after the breakpoints are pinned. The mechanism of balancing selection --- overdominance,
associative overdominance, frequency dependence, or a moving optimum under fluctuating selection --- is not
resolved by these data; a shelf-spawning squid whose paralarvae ride the Gulf Stream is a plausible setting
for temporally fluctuating selection on $\sim$100~ky glacial cycles, but this remains a hypothesis.

\subsection{Demographic history: strong expansion, not multiple mergers}\label{sec:demog}
Genome-wide, \illex{} shows $\pi\approx0.0093$ and Tajima's $D\approx-2$ in essentially 100\% of windows ---
an unambiguous, genome-wide signature of population expansion (\cref{tab:demog}, \cref{fig:demog},
\cref{fig:diversity}).
A moments fit to the folded SFS gives a growth history from an ancestral $\Ne=\text{547{,}928}$ to a present
$\Ne=\text{6{,}808{,}096}$ over $\text{769{,}519}$ generations ($\sim$12-fold; generations $\approx$ years for
this annual species), and this history reproduces both the observed $\pi$ and a full-SFS Tajima's $D=-1.92$
under forward simulation, ruling out a haploid/diploid scaling error. Stairway~Plot~2 and GONE2 concur on
strong recent expansion to $\Ne$ in the $1.5$--$1.7\times10^{7}$ range. Because sweepstakes reproduction
would mimic expansion by inflating rare variants, we tested the coalescent class directly: the observed
folded SFS is fit far better by a Kingman-plus-growth model than by any multiple-merger (Beta) coalescent
(shape-deviation $L_1=0.036$ vs a best-$\alpha$ $0.108$), and contemporary $\Ne$ estimates from the SFS and
from linkage disequilibrium agree --- both inconsistent with a strong multiple-merger process.

\subsection{The recombination landscape}\label{sec:recomb}
A deep-learning recombination-map method applied per sex gives an autosomal rate of $\sim$0.21--0.24~cM/Mb
(male $0.204$--$0.213$, female $0.288$--$0.290$, sex-averaged $\sim$0.244; Supplementary Fig.~S10). Because the
estimator reads linkage disequilibrium, it recovers the sex-\emph{averaged} rate, and heterochiasmy is not
identifiable from it (a male/female autosomal split estimates the same quantity; the apparent gap was an
artifact of unequal sample sizes). The Z chromosome shows a sex-biased rate, and a sex-averaged, boundary-
corrected genome map underlies the sweep-scan calibration.

\subsection{Diversity and gene-linked background selection}\label{sec:bgs}
Nucleotide diversity is reduced in gene-dense regions by 20--30\% ($\pi$ correlates with local coding density
at $r=-0.31$), a moderate background-selection effect that is gene-density-driven rather than recombination-
driven (recombination is nearly uniform, \cref{sec:recomb}). A model-based $B$-map could not be fit under this
regime (the classical approximation overflows where the deleterious mutation rate exceeds the selection
coefficient), so we use local coding fraction as a background-selection covariate and control for it explicitly
in the sweep scan (\cref{sec:sweep}). Because the callset is unphased, all diversity statistics are
site-frequency-spectrum-based ($\pi$, Watterson's $\theta$, Tajima's $D$); haplotype-homozygosity statistics
are degenerate on these data and are not used.

\subsection{Candidate adaptive genes (McDonald--Kreitman)}\label{sec:mk}
McDonald--Kreitman tests against the \coin{} outgroup across 20{,}999 genes give a genome-wide proportion of
adaptive substitutions $\alpha\approx0.34$--0.64. The single most strongly and significantly adaptive gene is
the splicing factor \textit{SF3B4} ($\alpha=0.64$; a single-copy gene by our annotation). Per-gene power is
limited --- a genome-wide $\alpha$ of this size resolves cleanly to only a handful of genes --- so we treat the
MK results as complementary to, rather than overlapping with, the recent-sweep scan below, which detects a
different (recent, often non-coding) timescale of selection. The second congener used here, \textit{I.\
argentinus}, is a striking comparator in its own right: it is recently diverged from \illex{} (roughly an
order of magnitude fewer fixed coding differences than \coin{}; Supplementary Table~S3) yet carries
near-identical genome-wide diversity ($\pi=0.0092$ by genotype-likelihood estimation on the same accessible
sites, versus $0.0093$ in \illex{} --- within 1\%) --- two large-$N_e$, expansion-shaped congeners of matched
standing variation, a comparison the single-species differentiation survey could not make.

\subsection{A genome-wide scan for selective sweeps}\label{sec:sweep}
We scanned for recent positive selection with a five-class site-frequency/diversity-based deep-learning
classifier (neutral, hard, soft, linked-hard, linked-soft; 1.1~Mb feature windows of eleven 100~kb
sub-windows), trained on forward and coalescent simulations under the fitted growth demography
(\cref{fig:selection}).

\paragraph{Two methodological findings shape the scan.}
First, a training/scanning feature mismatch --- the model was initially trained on phased-haplotype
simulation features but applied to unphased genotypes --- inflated linkage-based features by an order of
magnitude and had to be corrected by retraining on the same unphased feature extractor used to scan; the
final full-SFS model reaches 81\% class accuracy. Second, and more fundamentally, even the corrected model
\emph{over-calls} sweeps on the real genome: real neutral windows (accessible, $\geq$5~kb from any gene) carry
3--8-fold more \emph{within-window spatial heterogeneity} in $\pi$ and $\theta$ than the simulations, even
though their marginal SFS matches ($D\approx-2$). We ruled out missing-data structure and sample
incompleteness as the cause; the residual is real per-sub-window diversity variation (mutation-rate variation
plus background selection) that a uniform-$\theta$ coalescent does not reproduce. We therefore use the
classifier as a \textbf{stratified empirical-outlier detector} --- ranking a sweep score ($p_\mathrm{hard}+
p_\mathrm{soft}$) within strata of callable fraction and coding density so that a window is an outlier only
relative to windows of similar accessibility and gene density --- corroborated by a second, LD-footprint
method. We also show that an inherited minor-allele-frequency filter was a mistake under strong expansion
(it discards the rare-variant excess that carries both the expansion and the sweep signal) and move to the
full spectrum on both the simulation and empirical sides.

\paragraph{Two tiers of sweep calls.}
We report sweeps at two levels of stringency (Supplementary Fig.~S13). \textbf{Tier~1} is the diploSHIC
hard/soft classification itself, spatially resolved by a recombination-aware Markov decoder --- a hidden
Markov model over the per-100~kb-window classes whose neutral/linked/centre emissions are the
prior-corrected class posteriors and whose dwell lengths follow the $\sim$400~kb sweep footprint scaled by
the ReLERNN recombination map (Supplementary Fig.~S13a). Because the genome reads as $\sim$91\% linked-selected
under strong expansion, the decoder's role is spatial coherence: it keeps hard/soft calls that lie in
contiguous sweep centres and discards spatially isolated ones. This reduces the raw calls from 1{,}758 to
717 windows (658 localised calls; 459 hard, 199 soft; 35 on chromosomes~2 and~42), retaining the
higher-outlier windows (median empirical-outlier percentile 0.98 vs 0.95 for discarded calls;
Supplementary Fig.~S13b). \textbf{Tier~2} is the subset independently corroborated by two LD/SFS-footprint methods
(RAiSD, SweepFinder2): the 34 cross-method-concordant regions. The two tiers agree strongly --- 89\% of
windows at concordant loci are decoded as sweep centres versus 2.9\% genome-wide (odds ratio 282,
$p<10^{-46}$), and 30 of the 34 concordant regions contain a Tier-1 centre (Supplementary Fig.~S13c) --- so the
conservative concordance set is a high-purity subset of the classifier calls, not a disjoint list. Because
the decoder could not be simulation-calibrated at this genome's recombination density (a dense-ancestry
bottleneck; see Methods), its dwell parameters are set from the known sweep footprint and the recombination
map and validated post-hoc by this concordance; we therefore treat Tier~1 as a ranked candidate set and
Tier~2 as the high-confidence calls.

\paragraph{The Tier-1 set is enriched for neural-developmental and RNA-regulatory function.}
Independently of the cross-method concordance, the 658 Tier-1 calls overlap 894 genes (476 with GO
annotation; one-third of the calls fall in gene-poor, often transposon-rich windows and are excluded from the
enrichment). A gene-ontology analysis (Fisher's exact test, Benjamini--Hochberg FDR, against the
annotated-gene background on the scanned chromosomes) returns 124 terms at FDR $<0.05$, dominated by two
coherent themes: \textbf{neural development} (neurogenesis, neuron differentiation, cell-projection and
axon morphogenesis; 1.5--1.8-fold) and \textbf{transcriptional/RNA-regulatory control} (regulation of RNA
metabolic and biosynthetic process, transcription). The enrichment is carried entirely by the hard-sweep
calls (99 enriched terms); the disjoint soft-sweep set has none at FDR $<0.05$ and is underpowered. These
themes recover and statistically reinforce the smaller Tier-2 concordant set --- whose genes (the apelin
receptor, \textit{ZEB2}, \textit{Rab3}, \textit{cept1}) fall squarely within them --- indicating that the
recent-sweep target in \illex{} is a neural-developmental and gene-regulatory program, as expected for a
behaviourally and neurally complex, fast-developing cephalopod. Screening the Tier-1 calls by background
selection --- the same per-region test used for the concordant set, retaining only calls whose local $\pi$
falls below the background-selection expectation given flanking coding density --- keeps \textbf{291 of the
658} (237 hard, 54 soft). The enrichment not only survives this filter but \emph{strengthens}: 152 terms at
FDR $<0.05$, with the neural-development and RNA-regulatory folds rising throughout (e.g.\ generation of
neurons $1.6\!\to\!1.9$, transcription by RNA polymerase~II $1.7\!\to\!2.1$), and a \textbf{photoreception}
signal (response to light and radiation, $\sim$3-fold) emerging --- a coherent sensory-and-neural adaptive
program that is not an artefact of reduced diversity in gene-dense regions.

\paragraph{Candidate sweeps.}
The scan yields 216 candidate windows and \textbf{34 regions with cross-method concordance}; a per-region
background-selection test (comparing local $\pi$ to that expected from flanking coding density) isolates
\textbf{11 high-confidence sweep candidates} in which diversity is reduced beyond the background-selection
expectation. These carry a biologically coherent set of cephalopod-orthologous genes spanning neural and
developmental function (\textit{Rab3}, \textit{ZEB2}), cardiovascular/vasculature signalling (\textit{Aplnr}),
genome-maintenance and stress response (\textit{MACROD2}), and membrane/sphingolipid metabolism (\textit{cept1};
and, at a locus the annotation had missed, a \emph{ceramide phosphoethanolamine synthase}) --- functions
consistent with a fast, migratory, cold-water, neurally complex, short-lived animal (\cref{tab:sweep}).
The single most distinctive autosomal candidate is a \textbf{cephalopod-lineage-specific gene under a hard
sweep} on chromosome~16 (a tandem-exon transcript with no functional annotation whose direct translation
matches an uncharacterised protein family in the bobtail squid \textit{Euprymna}, $E=2\times10^{-53}$).
Fine-scale localisation confirms all 11 candidates as recent sweeps by the sign of Tajima's $D$ at the
diversity valley, and reassigns two apparent gene-desert signals to transposable-element regions (flagged as
lower confidence) and one candidate's target to a neighbouring gene. There is no overlap between the sweep
candidates and the MK-adaptive genes, as expected for their different timescales.

\paragraph{The Z chromosome.}
Because males are Z-diploid and females Z-haploid, the Z was scanned separately in the 330 confident ZZ
males, using a model retrained at the matched sample size (the classifier's features are sample-size
dependent) and a formal adequacy gate to control for the Z's reduced effective size; the male-only screen was
independently validated against the 289 haploid females, whose per-window diversity tracks the males' at
$r=0.996$. Three credible, gene-localised sweeps survive fine-scale triage, all confirmed as conserved
cephalopod genes by direct homology: \textbf{Pasilla/Nova-1} (a neuronal alternative-splicing regulator;
92\% identity to \textit{Octopus}), \textbf{Smaug/samd4a} (a germline RNA-binding translational repressor),
and \textbf{Esrrg} (an estrogen-related receptor, with an adjacent likely-\textit{cis}-regulatory lncRNA;
97.5\% identity to \textit{Octopus}) --- a coherent RNA-regulation-and-hormone-signalling signature for a sex
chromosome. Notably, the \textit{Amnionless}/``Zfest'' sex-determination locus falls just outside the swept
valley and shows only purifying selection: it is a linked passenger, not the target.

\paragraph{The inversion is not a sweep outlier.}
Completing the scan across all 45 autosomes plus the Z, chromosome~2 yields \emph{zero} concordant sweeps: the
recombination-map method is broadly elevated across the inversion and the classifier over-calls there, but the
two do not co-localise into focal sweep concordance --- exactly the pattern expected for a balancing-selected
supergene rather than a swept region, and correctly filtered by the concordance requirement.

\section{Discussion}

\illex{} emerges as a large-$\Ne$, expansion-dominated system whose genome carries a single, dramatic axis of
structure --- a balancing-selected chromosome-2 supergene --- against an otherwise unstructured, weakly
background-selected background. Where an earlier survey \citep{baker2025} described this structure, our
contribution is to explain it and to look past it: to show that the chr2 inversion is a selected, datable
supergene with a specific frequency history, and to resolve the genome-wide sweep landscape --- including on
the sex chromosome --- that a differentiation scan does not reveal. Three results deserve emphasis.

The inversion is the clearest case. Its rare, derived arrangement is more diverse than its frequency allows,
which forces a history in which it was once common and is now declining; two mutation-rate-free arguments and
a barrier-aware coalescent fit converge on an $\sim$850~ky-old polymorphism, coincident with the onset of the
population expansion, that has been actively removed over the last $\sim$50--100~ky. That the age survived a
complete reversal of the arrangement polarisation --- because it is carried by the polarisation-symmetric
divergence between arrangements --- is a useful robustness check that few inversion-age estimates can claim.
What the data do not resolve is the mechanism of balancing selection or the identity of its target: the block
is gene-poor and differentiated as a unit, with no single locus standing out, consistent with selection on a
co-adapted arrangement rather than a single gene.

The demographic result matters methodologically as much as biologically. A sweepstakes-reproduction species is
exactly where one expects multiple-merger coalescents to masquerade as expansion; testing the coalescent class
directly, rather than assuming Kingman, is what licenses every downstream age and selection estimate here.

Finally, the sweep scan is a cautionary and constructive example. Simulation-trained per-window classifiers
are increasingly standard, but on a strongly expanding, unphased, missing-data-rich genome they over-call
because real neutral sequence is spatially more heterogeneous than tractable neutral simulations. Rather than
hide this behind a threshold, we quantified it and let it dictate the framing: a stratified empirical-outlier
scan with cross-method concordance and an explicit background-selection filter. The resulting candidate set is
small and biologically coherent --- neural, RNA-regulatory, membrane-lipid, and stress-response genes, a
lineage-specific novelty under a hard sweep, and a sex-chromosome cluster of RNA-regulation and hormone-
signalling loci --- and is offered as hypothesis-generating rather than as a list of confirmed targets.

\section{Limitations}
The callset is unphased, so no haplotype-homozygosity statistics are available; sweep detection relies on the
SFS and diversity alone. The simulation-to-real neutral mismatch that drives classifier over-calling is real
and unresolved at the level of absolute neutral calling --- our empirical-outlier framing is a principled
response, not a cure --- and it also defeats a within-arrangement SFS approach to the inversion. Every
inversion age scales inversely with the mutation rate, and $\mu=3\times10^{-9}$ is the weakest external input;
an independent calibration (e.g.\ a karyotyped congener) is the clearest route to a mutation-rate-free age.
The corroborating LD-footprint method is broad and the abandoned frequency-spectrum method was unreliable on
these data. Candidate sweep genes rest on homology to distant cephalopod models and on cross-method
concordance; they are candidates, not confirmed adaptive targets.

\section{Methods}\label{sec:methods}
Full parameter values, software versions, and command-level provenance are in
Supplementary Table~S1 and in the project repository.

\paragraph{Data.}
The ingroup is the 350-individual \illex{} subset of the baker\_2025 whole-genome callset (of 633 sequenced),
mapped to the \texttt{Illex\_F24} primary assembly (45 numbered chromosomes plus the Z). Genotypes are unphased
and the VCF is variants-only (no invariant sites), so diversity is computed from genotype likelihoods off the
BAMs rather than from called genotypes. \coin{} is the divergence/MK outgroup. An accessibility mask restricts
all analyses to callable sites.

\paragraph{Diversity, SFS, and demography.}
Site-frequency spectra, Watterson's $\theta$, per-window $\pi$ and Tajima's $D$, and per-karyotype spectra were
computed with ANGSD (\texttt{-doSaf}/\texttt{realSFS}). Demographic history was inferred with Stairway~Plot~2,
moments, and GONE2, and validated by forward simulation reproducing the observed $\pi$ and $D$. Windowed
$\pi$, $\dxy$, $\Fst$, and PCA used pg\_gpu.

\paragraph{Karyotyping and the inversion.}
Karyotypes were called by local-PCA/lostruct and a Gaussian-mixture model. Arrangement polarisation used an
AnchorWave \coin{}$\leftrightarrow$\illex{} whole-genome alignment restricted to non-reference-biased sites.
The inversion's age and frequency trajectory were fit with \textbf{msinv} v0.3.5, an ARG-based coalescent
simulator that enforces cross-karyotype recombination barriers and outputs tree sequences, implementing the
hulls-based ancestral-material tracking of \citet{kelleher2016}; it was validated against SLiM, msprime, and
discoal. \textbf{All coalescent simulations underlying the inversion age and decline inference were generated
with msinv}, and the complete chr2-inversion analysis --- the fitting code, the simulation configuration, and
every per-analysis output and figure --- is housed as a dedicated \illex{} module within the msinv repository
(\texttt{files/illex/}, \texttt{results/illex/}). Simulations used the fitted growth demography,
$\mu=3\times10^{-9}$, and $r=1.977\times10^{-9}$; statistics were computed on the differentiated body and
rescaled from a 37-kb simulated inversion with length-invariance verified over a 25-fold range. Breakpoints were pinned by combining arrangement
differentiation with the AnchorWave alignment; missed genes were recovered by DIAMOND \texttt{blastx} against
seven cephalopod proteomes, with transposable elements annotated by EarlGrey and transcripts by StringTie.

\paragraph{Recombination map.}
Per-sex recombination maps were built with ReLERNN (SIMULATE$\to$TRAIN$\to$PREDICT$\to$BSCORRECT), dropping
individuals with no calls on a chromosome and never forcing diploid genotype calls.

\paragraph{Selective-sweep scan.}
The classifier was diploSHIC (five classes, 1.1~Mb windows of eleven 100~kb sub-windows, twelve diploid
statistics per sub-window), trained on SLiM~5.2 forward simulations (recapitated with msprime) and pure-msprime
neutral simulations under the fitted growth demography, at the empirical sample size and with realistic
structured missingness. The model was trained on the same unphased feature extractor (\texttt{fvecVcf}) used
to scan, on the full spectrum (no MAF filter). It was used as a stratified empirical-outlier detector (score
$p_\mathrm{hard}+p_\mathrm{soft}$ ranked within callable-fraction $\times$ coding-density strata), corroborated
by RAiSD; SweepFinder2 and a $B$-value calculator were evaluated but not used for reported results. The Z
chromosome was scanned in males only with a model retrained at $n=330$ and passed a comparative feature-adequacy
gate, then validated against the haploid females. Candidate genes were annotated with EnTAP and screened by
DIAMOND against cephalopod proteomes; a per-region background-selection test compared local $\pi$ to the
flanking-coding-density expectation. The per-window classifications were spatially resolved into Tier-1
calls with a recombination-aware hidden Markov model (neutral / linked / sweep-centre states; emissions are
the prior-corrected collapsed class posteriors; dwell lengths set from the $\sim$400~kb sweep footprint
scaled by the ReLERNN sex-averaged map, with a constant-rate fallback on chromosomes~2 and~42). A
simulation calibration of the decoder was attempted but proved infeasible on this genome: at $\Ne$-scale
recombination the recapitated ancestral recombination graphs carry $\sim$3.4$\times10^{5}$ marginal trees
per 2.5~Mb, making the tree-sequence genotype ascertainment for the training scan prohibitively slow
($>$2.7~h per segment). The decoder's dwell parameters are therefore design-informed from the sweep
footprint and the recombination map rather than fit to simulations, and are validated post-hoc by the
89\%/2.9\% Tier-1--Tier-2 concordance (Supplementary Fig.~S13).

\paragraph{McDonald--Kreitman.}
MK tests used mkado against the \coin{} outgroup over 20{,}999 genes.
